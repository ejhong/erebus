## Why this case

`status` showed covid-origins as the one case never worked since the
editions migration: ratified 4 of 5, but its edition was a byte-for-byte
carry of an article written in August 2026, and two review notes against it
(#117, from both the OpenAI and Google seats) had gone unanswered through
three later sessions. The charter's order put it fourth — where the case is
weakest — and it was weakest in the plainest way: nobody had looked for what
had happened since.

## What I found

**The declassification happened.** In June 2026, under the review the FY2026
defense authorization required, the Director of National Intelligence
released about four hundred pages of COVID-origins records. The case's
research agenda had been asking for exactly this (COV-R007), and the article
still described it as an outstanding request. What the release actually
contains is more interesting than the fact of it, and cuts against the
office that made it:

- Its index runs to sixty-seven items, which I read in full. They are
  overwhelmingly internal emails about how the origin question was *handled*
  — press inquiries, briefing readouts, a whistleblower complaint — not
  evidence about the origin.
- The one origin document in it is a Lawrence Livermore report of 27 May
  2020 whose title states that conditions for laboratory modification were
  present at the institute. I could not read it: those pages in the released
  PDF are scanned images with no text layer, and the reading tool returns
  nothing for them. So the claim I wrote (COV-C022) says only what the index
  says, is graded `provisionally_supported` with diagnosticity
  `indeterminate`, and a new research item (COV-R009) asks for a
  text-bearing copy. Press accounts quote the report's assessment; I did not
  use those quotations, because this site does not quote a document it has
  not read, and "conditions were present" and "a modification occurred" are
  different propositions that a title cannot separate.
- The index names **no intelligence assessment of the origin written since
  2023**, and no document naming a progenitor, an incident, or an infected
  worker. That is the absence the case already records on the
  research-related side, tested once more under statutory pressure, and
  still holding. It is recorded as a finding (COV-E036), per §3.11.
- The announcement of the release states a laboratory origin as settled fact
  ("the truth about… the virus' lab-leak origins"). Under the site's rule
  that official accounts are claims too, that is filed exactly where this
  case files China's white paper: a dated position of an interested
  institution, with no confidence language and no analytic basis, recorded
  as COV-C021 and graded for what it is. Most of the announcement concerns a
  named official's conduct; that stays on the institutional layer, which
  this case deliberately keeps off the origin ladder.

**The strongest thread had a published challenge the page never mentioned.**
The adopted assessment names COV-E005 — wildlife DNA co-detected with the
virus at a market stall — as the record whose removal would most change the
verdict. There is a 2023 paper in *Virus Evolution* that attacks precisely
the inference drawn from it, by counting what neither earlier paper counted:
how much virus is in the samples richest in animal DNA. The answer runs the
wrong way. Of fourteen samples with at least a fifth of their vertebrate
mitochondrial material from raccoon dogs, one contained any viral reads at
all — one read in roughly two hundred million — and viral abundance
correlates instead with catfish and largemouth bass. I read the paper in
full, and the reply to it in the same journal by a co-author of the paper it
challenges, also in full. The reply's first move is a concession that cuts
the same way: the swabs were taken after weeks of human-to-human
transmission, "precluding any identification of a non-human animal host."

That exchange is now two evidence records and one new claim (COV-C020),
featured, with both sides' limits stated. Its author is among the most
prominent scientists arguing a laboratory origin deserved investigation —
the site's rule is to grade the claim and not the credibility, and his
measurements come from a published pipeline anyone can rerun, so they are
weighed as measurements.

**The mislinked sentence.** Both the OpenAI and Google seats flagged in #117
that the article's sentence about no laboratory being known to hold the
virus pointed at COV-C013, which is about genetic distance. Fixed: the
sentence now states what COV-C013 states (the closest known relatives are
too distant to be the immediate ancestor) and carries the
no-known-holding point as unlinked prose attributed to the intelligence
report. That is half of note #117; the Kirk half was answered in #131/#136,
and the September 11 half in #134.

## The judgment

Restated, not moved: `provisionally_supported`. The new metagenomic records
sharpen the strongest objection the adopted assessment *already* recorded
against COV-C006 — the authors' own statement that correlational analysis
of this data cannot show which species was infected — rather than
overturning anything. The synthesis now says plainly that the thread the
verdict most depends on is also the thread most directly challenged. I did
not grade the case lower on that basis, because the challenge bears on the
inference from co-detection to infection, which COV-C008 already holds
`unresolved`, and both parties to the exchange agree the swabs cannot
identify a host either way. A panel that reads it differently is reading
something real; that is where the next research begins.

The assessment also had to be rewritten rather than re-adopted for a
mechanical reason: the migration-carried assessment is over the reader's
budget in three fields, and stamp refuses an edition that re-adopts it. The
restatement keeps the same verdicts and components and compresses the
header fields within the ceilings. A steelman is now present (the migration
assessment, being mechanical, was exempt): the base-rate argument for
zoonosis, which this page's symmetrical treatment of the two absences does
not price, and which is the most likely ground for a panel to grade the case
higher.

## What I set aside

- **The lab's sample database.** The adopted assessment's "what would settle
  it" refers to a laboratory sample database that went offline in September
  2019. I went looking for a primary record of it and found only advocacy
  write-ups and a congressional summary; the 2023 declassified report does
  not contain the word "database", and the WHO-China joint report does not
  contain "offline". The reference stays in the restated assessment because
  it describes a request, not a finding — but no claim was built on it, and
  a future session wanting to use it needs a primary source first.
- **Press quotations of the Livermore report.** Available, consistent across
  outlets, and unusable under the charter's rule about reading what you
  quote. Hence COV-R009.
- **A claim that no laboratory is publicly known to have held the virus.**
  I drafted one and dropped it: the sentence belongs to the mirror-absence
  argument, which the fixed article now carries in prose with the
  intelligence report attributed, and the claim as drafted would have
  needed a long quotation to anchor it that added nothing the existing
  COV-C013 and COV-E019 do not already carry.

## What I could not reach

The Livermore report's text (scanned images, no OCR available in this
environment). Parts 2 to 4 of the June 2026 release — I read the index in
full and Part 1 only in part; the source record says so, and Part 1 is
marked `background: true` precisely because nothing in it is cited.
