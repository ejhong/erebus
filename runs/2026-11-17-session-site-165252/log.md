## What I found

**1. The government's answer on the confession (ECF No. 130).**

- **It concedes what the agents did.** In its own words, the agents "downplayed the severity of the crimes", by the Reid approach. They said confessing would help him, and they told him his family "might not have to" be brought to Washington. It concedes "there is no question" he was distressed when confronted. It argues all of this was lawful.
- **Its answer on the family.** It says the family were already under subpoena to appear the next day, and were released after he talked. It quotes the defense's own motion conceding the agents never said "arrest". New claim PIPE-C097.
- **Regrades.** PIPE-C042 (his distress) rises from provisionally to well supported, since the adverse party concedes it. PIPE-C041 stays provisionally supported, with its reasoning rewritten: the tactics are now common ground, and "implied threats" is the defense's characterization.
- **It cites the confession to the transcript.** It cites each post-admission detail to Tr. 103–153, as about an hour and a quarter of account; earlier filings said an hour and a half. It says his card records show a January 14, 2018 purchase of Lilly Miller sulfur dust, the brand he named for the powder (new claim PIPE-C091).
  - This is the first detail of the confession the prosecution says its records confirm, so it bears directly on PIPE-C040, the crux of the confession.
  - The brief does not say who named the brand first.
  - Its own account of Tr. 88 shows the agents listed timers, connectors, pipe, end caps, clips and wire before his admission, but not sulfur. That adds parts to what PIPE-C080 records the agents had named.
  - **PIPE-C040 stays unresolved.** The answer is on Tr. 104–105, which I did not read. The transcript is a scan read by eye. A reading of the post-admission pages is the broad audit PIPE-R003 asks for, and under AGENTS §3.12 it should be registered as a study before the pages are read. R003 now says so and names where to begin. I moved it to second in the crux order.
- **New assertions, from records not public.** Each is a new claim graded provisionally supported:
  - a deleted copy of the Anarchist Cookbook, dated 2016, on a thumb drive (PIPE-C092);
  - 2012 searches for how to make a pipe bomb, under a profile named "Brian", on a drive in a family member's room (PIPE-C093);
  - his computer's operating system reinstalled hours before his arrest (PIPE-C094);
  - a recorded jail call of December 12, 2025, in which he called "what I did four years ago" the dumbest thing he had done (PIPE-C095).

  On the jail call, I also record what the same footnote says: his parents at once told him he had done nothing, and he agreed. Including only the inculpatory half would misstate the government's own account.
- **Its expert.** A neuropsychologist it retained reports no functional impairment and anxiety that largely disappeared after his admission (PIPE-C096). Her report (ECF No. 130-3) is a scan without a text layer, so she is quoted only as the brief quotes her.
- **PIPE-C033 (the alibi).** It gains one undermining record: by the government's account, Cole later said he attended no protest that night. It is graded weak, because it comes from the disputed part of the interview.

**2. The insider account (ECF No. 128, read in full).** The defense's motion of November 2 says the FBI's inquiry into the former officer collected:

- no cell-site data for her numbers;
- no warrant for her Apple account;
- no home-camera footage;
- no extraction of the phone she carried in January 2021.

It also says the FBI's first review of the phone it did image missed eleven geotagged photographs, caught only on a re-review the defense prompted. This is new claim PIPE-C098, graded provisionally supported: it is the defense's description of government letters that are not public, and the government has not answered in public. It bears on how much the government's "found no evidence" (PIPE-C088) can carry. It is not evidence about her.
