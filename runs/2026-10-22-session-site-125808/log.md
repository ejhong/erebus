## What I found

- **The August 28, 2025 opinion itself** (S.D.N.Y. No. 03-md-01570, ECF No. 11182, 45 pp.). I read it through the reading tool from a legal publisher's copy of the filed PDF. Every page carries the court's ECF header, and the court's later stay order quotes the opinion's p. 45 in the same words. What it records:
  - The Kingdom does not dispute that one of the two employees helped the hijackers with their apartment, helped fill out their application forms and signed the lease as guarantor. It also does not dispute the dates and places of his meetings with them. It denies that the second man helped them at all (SEP-E041).
  - The judge found it "more likely than not" that the meetings were not chance, and found a "reasonable inference" that the help followed "KSA's instructions". The judge wrote that the evidence "creates a high probability" as to both men's roles "and the related role of their employer", and rejected the Kingdom's explanations, among them coincidence and school algebra for the airplane drawing (SEP-E042).
  - The same opinion calls this "a preliminary finding concerning jurisdictional inquiries only". It leaves disputed facts and the credibility of witnesses to trial (SEP-E043).
- **The stay is real, and now sourced.** The CourtListener index of the MDL docket led to the order of November 5, 2025 (ECF No. 11383), which grants the Kingdom's motion to stay all proceedings involving it pending its interlocutory appeal. The notice of appeal was filed September 8, 2025 (SEP-E045).
- **The appeal was argued on October 7, 2026, and the court has not ruled** (AP, SEP-E046). The same report gives the Kingdom's position: it denies any involvement and says it was a partner against al-Qaeda (SEP-E044). The page's line that nothing was proceeding anywhere was wrong, and is corrected.
- **The mislinked sentence.** The exhibits sentence now links a new claim, SEP-C030: the Moussaoui court admitted 1,202 exhibits and published all but seven. SEP-E038 moves to that claim, with a comment in the record saying why.

## The judgment

I wrote a new draft assessment, which is why the log names the case for the panel. One verdict changes: **SEP-C023** (a Saudi state institution knowingly supported the plot) moves from **unresolved to mixed**, with low confidence. A neutral court has now weighed substantial evidence for the claim after adversarial discovery. But its finding is preliminary and jurisdictional, rests on exhibits I have not read, is on appeal, and reaches less than the claim does. Scope of employment and deliberate indifference toward two hijackers are not an institution knowingly supporting the plot. The reasoning, the treatment and the article all say this. I considered provisionally supported and rejected it for that gap. I considered keeping unresolved and rejected that too: a court's adversarial weighing is evidence, and the scale's "unresolved" would hide it. SEP-C022 stays well supported. One of the two men's help is now undisputed by the Kingdom, but the other's is denied. Every other verdict is carried over unchanged.

The header fields were over the reader's budget as migrated, so I restated them within it without changing what they say. The assessment now carries a steelman, which it did not have before. It states the strongest case against the assessment: that the institutions in question hold or destroyed the records that would test it. It also says which parts of that case the assessment cannot answer.
