## 1. [study] The S1/S2 junction table: polybasic cleavage motifs across sampled coronaviruses

**Question / truth condition:** Across a frozen set of published coronavirus genomes (all sarbecoviruses with sequenced spike, plus merbecoviruses, embecoviruses such as HKU1/OC43, and alphacoronaviruses), how many independently sampled natural lineages carry a polybasic (multibasic) motif at the S1/S2 junction, and in how many of those did the motif arise by insertion relative to the nearest sampled relative rather than by substitution? Answerable by aligning the S1/S2 region and tabulating motif, insertion length, and nucleotide-level mechanism, with inclusion criteria and reference alignments fixed before counting.

**Closest existing:** COV-C011, COV-C012, COV-C013, COV-E013 — C011 states only that the motif is absent from SARS-CoV-2's closest sampled sarbecovirus relatives, and E013 repeats that for the Laotian viruses; C012 states that no genomic feature is presently accepted as diagnostic of engineering, without exhibiting the comparative base rate. None of them tabulates how often polybasic S1/S2 motifs, and insertions creating them, occur in naturally sampled coronaviruses — the quantity that determines how surprising SARS-CoV-2's insertion is.

**What it would settle:** If polybasic S1/S2 motifs recur across multiple independently sampled natural coronavirus lineages and several arose by insertion, the presence of the motif carries little evidential weight on its own and the argument must rest on the specific sarbecovirus context. If SARS-CoV-2's is the only insertion-derived polybasic S1/S2 motif in the sampled coronavirus record, the feature is a genuine outlier that any origin account must explain.
