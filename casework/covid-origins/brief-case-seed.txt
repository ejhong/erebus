   Purpose of this document. This report is a comprehensive evidence seed for the Erebus case
   on the origin of the COVID-19 pandemic. It decomposes the controversy into atomic claims, maps
   the evidence for and against each claim with exact provenance, covers the circumstantial record
   produced by recent congressional hearings and document releases (2023–2026, including the Fauci
   hearings), identifies where the disagreement actually lives, and proposes the tests that would settle
   each crux. It does not deliver a verdict: as of August 2026, no definitive evidence establishes either
   a natural spillover or a research-related incident, and every major institutional assessment carries
   explicit uncertainty qualifiers.



Executive Summary
Six years after SARS-CoV-2 emerged in Wuhan, the question of how it entered the human population
remains formally unresolved, but the evidentiary landscape is no longer symmetric in the way it is
often described. The direct, positive evidence — epidemiological clustering, phylodynamics, and
market metagenomics — mostly points toward zoonotic spillover associated with the Huanan Seafood
Wholesale Market: 66% of the first 41 hospitalized patients had direct market exposure, early cases
without any market link lived significantly closer to the market than market-linked cases, and two early
viral lineages are consistent with two spillover events in November–December 2019 (Science | AAAS) .
The circumstantial evidence — proximity of China’s foremost SARS-coronavirus laboratory, a 2018
funding proposal to insert furin cleavage sites into bat coronaviruses in Wuhan, documented biosafety
shortcomings, an offline sequence database, and a documented pattern of private-versus-public divergence
among senior scientists and oﬀicials — points toward, or at least fails to exclude, a research-related
incident (archive.org) .

The recent hearings and document releases have materially changed the record on conduct, not on
virology. The 2024–2026 House and Senate investigations documented that senior NIAID adviser David
Morens used private email to evade FOIA and described deleting federal records; that the authors of “The
Proximal Origin of SARS-CoV-2” privately assigned non-trivial probabilities to a lab origin (Andersen
30%, Holmes 20%→10%) even while their paper said no laboratory scenario was plausible; and that
Anthony Fauci, subpoenaed before the Senate on 29 July 2026, invoked the Fifth Amendment 111 times
and was held in contempt of Congress on 6 August 2026, with a referral sent to the Department of Justice
(house.gov) . None of this is evidence about where the virus came from — but it is direct evidence about
the reliability of the early “consensus” narrative, which is itself one of the load-bearing claims in the case.

The decisive fact is structural: both hypotheses currently rest partly on absences. No infected
intermediate animal has been identified despite roughly 80,000 animals tested in China; no SARS-CoV-2
progenitor has been identified in any laboratory collection; the WHO’s 27-member SAGO panel concluded
in June 2025 that “the weight of available evidence … suggests zoonotic spillover” while explicitly stating
it could not even assess the laboratory hypothesis because China has not provided the underlying records
(Science | AAAS) . The case is therefore genuinely open — and its resolution depends on a short list of
specific, obtainable pieces of evidence enumerated in Section 9.

1. What is claimed
The case decomposes into two families of hypotheses, each with sub-variants that are frequently conflated
in public argument. Precision here matters, because much of the six-year controversy has consisted of
parties answering different claims.

Hypothesis family Z — zoonotic spillover (market-associated). SARS-CoV-2 evolved in bats
(and possibly intermediate mammal hosts), reached Wuhan through the wildlife trade, and spilled into
humans at or around the Huanan Seafood Wholesale Market in roughly November–December 2019, most
likely in two independent introductions corresponding to lineages A and B (Science | AAAS) . This is
the position of the mainstream virology literature (Worobey et al. and Pekar et al. in Science, July 2022;
Crits-Christoph et al. in Cell, September 2024) and of the WHO’s SAGO panel, which in June 2025
found “the weight of available evidence” supports zoonotic spillover “either directly from bats or through
an intermediate host,” while declining to rule anything else out (Cell Press) .

Hypothesis family L — research-related incident. SARS-CoV-2, or a close progenitor, was present
in a Wuhan laboratory — most plausibly the Wuhan Institute of Virology (WIV) — through field
collection or laboratory manipulation, and a researcher was infected and carried the virus into the city.
Sub-variants range from L1 (infection with an unmodified collected virus) through L2 (infection with a
virus altered by serial passage or reverse genetics, e.g. a furin-cleavage-site insertion of the type proposed
in the 2018 DEFUSE bid) to L3 (deliberate engineering/release), the last of which is rejected by the entire
US intelligence community and by virtually all scientists on both sides (FactCheck.org) . This family is
favored, with low-to-moderate confidence, by the FBI, the Department of Energy, the CIA (since January
2025), and Germany’s BND (since 2020, revealed 2025), and by the Republican majority of the House
Select Subcommittee’s December 2024 final report (ABC News) .

Two framing claims sit underneath the origin question and are independently contested. The first is
the “consensus” claim — that the scientific community converged on zoonosis through normal, data-
driven deliberation. The second is the suppression claim — that senior US oﬀicials and funded scientists
steered the early narrative away from a lab origin for reputational or institutional reasons. The 2024–
2026 documentary record bears directly on these meta-claims, and they are where the hearings matter
(senate.gov) .

2. The stakes and the state of play
The pandemic killed on the order of 15 million people in 2020–2021 by WHO excess-mortality esti-
mation (14.83 million, uncertainty interval 13.2–16.6 million), roughly 2.7 times the oﬀicially reported
death toll for that period, and reported deaths reached about 7.1 million by August 2025 — a figure
widely considered a large undercount (nih.gov) . Knowing whether this began with a wildlife trader’s
stall or a laboratory procedure determines which of two very different prevention regimes — wildlife-trade
regulation or biosafety and research oversight — the world should prioritize, which is why the question
remains institutionally alive rather than merely historical.

Public and institutional opinion have decoupled. By March 2023, American polls showed lab-leak belief
at 64–66% versus 16–22% for natural origin (Quinnipiac; Economist/YouGov), even as the mainstream
virology literature moved the other way (The Washington Post) . Within the US government there is no
consensus: four intelligence entities plus the National Intelligence Council lean natural (low confidence),
the FBI leans lab (moderate confidence), the Department of Energy and CIA lean lab (low confidence),
and the ODNI’s June 2023 declassified report found “no direct evidence” of a biosafety incident while
documenting the conditions that would make one plausible (FactCheck.org) . Internationally, Germany’s
BND reportedly assessed a lab accident at 80–95% probability as early as 2020 — a conclusion the Merkel
government did not publish — while China’s April 2025 white paper declares the lab leak “extremely
unlikely,” points instead at the United States, and declares the investigation “finished” (The Telegraph)
.

3. The scientific evidence for a market zoonosis
3.1 Early-case epidemiology points at the market

The earliest clinical description of the outbreak, Huang et al. in The Lancet (15 February 2020), covered
41 patients hospitalized by 2 January 2020, of whom 27 (66%) had direct exposure to the Huanan
market; the symptom onset of the first identified patient was 1 December 2019, and — a detail that has
fueled six years of argument — “no epidemiological link was found between the first patient and later
cases” (The Lancet) . The fuller WHO-mission case series found 55 of 168 early cases associated with
the market, and the Worobey et al. reanalysis established the subtler pattern that carries the statistical
weight: cases without any reported market link resided significantly closer to the market than cases who
worked there, which is the signature expected if the market was the exposure site rather than an artifact
of contact tracing, and excess pneumonia deaths rose first in the districts surrounding the market (Science
| AAAS) .

Worobey and colleagues further showed that SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples inside the
market were spatially concentrated in its western section, where live-mammal vendors operated, and that
susceptible species — raccoon dogs, civets, bamboo rats and others — were being sold there in late 2019
(Science | AAAS) . Ascertainment bias has been the principal counterargument, and the paper answers it
with several independent observations: all cases analyzed were hospitalized (so unlinked mild cases would
not have been found by neighborhood searches); oﬀicials became aware of market-linked cases both near
and far from the market simultaneously; and seroprevalence was later found highest in the districts around
the market (Science | AAAS) . This remains the single strongest positive evidentiary thread in the entire
case, and it is why the WHO SAGO panel, even while protesting Chinese non-cooperation, kept zoonosis
in front (WHO) .

3.2 Two lineages, two introductions

Pekar et al. (Science, July 2022) analyzed early genomic diversity and found that pre-February-2020
SARS-CoV-2 comprised two distinct lineages, A and B, separated by two nucleotide differences,
whose joint pattern is best explained by at least two separate cross-species transmission events:
lineage B entering humans around 18 November 2019 (95% HPD 23 October–8 December) and lineage
A within days to weeks afterward, with the lineage-B primary case preceding lineage A in 64.6% of
posterior samples (Science | AAAS) . The authors’ simulations did not support a single introduction
producing the observed pattern, and the analysis indicates the virus was unlikely to have circulated
widely in humans before November 2019 — a bound that matters because it constrains both a cryptic
pre-December community phase and a long pre-outbreak lab-incubation story (Science | AAAS) .

The two-introduction result is genuinely load-bearing for the zoonotic case — SAGO explicitly cited it
as the pattern “rather than what would be expected from a lab leak — which would be a single virus
introduction” (World Health Organization (WHO)) — and it is correspondingly contested. The inference
depends on rooting choices and on the assumption that the two lineages reflect two jumps rather than one
jump followed by early diversification and sampling gaps; the earliest lineage-A genomes lacked known
market contact but came from people who lived or stayed close by, which both sides read in their favor
(Science | AAAS) . This is a crux (Section 8): it is falsifiable in principle by additional early sequence
data — several hundred early-patient sequences held by China remain unpublished (Middle East Health)
.

3.3 The market’s animal evidence: “DNA and RNA ghosts”

The strongest market-specific forensic result came in September 2024, when Crits-Christoph, Débarre,
Worobey and colleagues published in Cell a metagenomic reanalysis of environmental swabs collected
in the market in early 2020. They found increased SARS-CoV-2 positivity near and within one wildlife
stall, wildlife DNA in all SARS-CoV-2-positive samples from that stall — including raccoon
dogs (the most abundant mammal in the wildlife-stall samples), masked palm civets, hoary bamboo rats
and Malayan porcupines — plus animal viruses known to infect those species, and market-linked viral
diversity “consistent with market emergence” (Cell Press) . Raccoon dogs are experimentally susceptible,
shed high titers, and carried SARS-CoV-1 in 2003; civets were the amplifying host of the first SARS
(ScienceDaily) .

The caveat is written into the paper’s provenance: “Many of the key animal species had been cleared
out before the Chinese CDC teams arrived, so we can’t have direct proof that the animals were infected
… We are seeing the DNA and RNA ghosts of these animals in the environmental samples” (Débarre)
(ScienceDaily) . Co-location of animal DNA and viral RNA on shared swabs establishes co-presence,
not infection, and the market had been closed, cleared and disinfected before systematic sampling on 12
January and later — the sampling itself was of surfaces and drains, not animals (ScienceDaily) . China’s
position, stated to the 2021 WHO mission, is that some 80,000 animals across species and provinces
were tested with no SARS-CoV-2 positives, and no animal sample from the market itself was positive
(Science | AAAS) . The honest summary: the market was saturated with the virus and with susceptible
animals in the same few stalls; whether the virus arrived in an animal, a trader, or a customer remains
undetermined from these data alone.

3.4 The genome: relatives, the furin cleavage site, and the CGG-CGG argument

The closest known relatives of SARS-CoV-2 are bat coronaviruses: RaTG13 (96.1–96.3% genome iden-
tity), sampled by the WIV in 2013 from a Tongguan mineshaft in Mojiang, Yunnan, and the BANAL


                                                                                      Generated by Kimi
viruses from Laos (BANAL-52, 96.8%), identified in 2020 (World Health Organization (WHO)) . These
are far too distant to be progenitors — RaTG13 differs from SARS-CoV-2 by roughly 1,100 nucleotides,
implying decades of independent evolution — and none possesses the virus’s most discussed feature, the
furin cleavage site (FCS): a 12-nucleotide insertion (TCCTCGGCGGGC) encoding a PRRA polyba-
sic motif at the spike S1/S2 junction that enhances cell entry and transmissibility (Science | AAAS) .
SARS-CoV-2 is the only one of more than 800 known sarbecoviruses with an FCS, and the insertion’s
two arginines are encoded by CGG-CGG, a codon doublet that accounts for only ~3% of arginine codons
in the virus (IntechOpen) .

Each side of the FCS argument is well developed. For engineering-suspicion: the FCS is absent from every
known close relative including the Laos/Vietnam viruses whose receptor-binding domains are otherwise
near-identical; CGG-CGG is rare in coronaviruses but common in human codon usage and mammalian
expression systems; Nobel laureate David Baltimore called it “the smoking gun for the origin of the
virus” before walking the phrase back; and — critically — inserting human-specific cleavage sites into
bat coronavirus spikes is precisely what the DEFUSE proposal described doing in Wuhan (Section 4.2)
(nih.gov) . For natural acquisition: FCS motifs exist across other coronavirus lineages (HKU1, OC43,
MERS-CoV and merbecoviruses), coronaviruses recombine promiscuously, the identical 12-nt sequence
occurs in bat coronavirus Bat-HKU9, and the SARS-CoV-2 FCS is suboptimal — an engineer choosing
from known sites would likely have used a more basic motif, per Susan Weiss and Stanley Perlman
(feedback.org) . SAGO’s June 2025 report sided with natural recombination (“can be explained by
natural recombination and mutations”), while a January 2026 peer-reviewed review concludes current
evidence “does not permit definitive attribution” of the FCS (World Health Organization (WHO)) . Both
can be true about the genome; they diverge about what the genome implies, and the genome alone will
not settle it.

3.5 The two absences: no animal host, no lab progenitor

The zoonotic case carries one conspicuous hole: unlike SARS-CoV-1 (civets identified within months)
and MERS (camels), no intermediate host carrying a SARS-CoV-2-like virus has been found after six
years and tens of thousands of tested animals — a point SAGO itself flagged as “surprising, considering
the many susceptible species present at [the market],” while cautioning that China’s animal testing has
reported zero positives, which sits oddly beside the documented reverse-zoonosis of the virus into mink,
deer and other species worldwide (World Health Organization (WHO)) . The absence is not dispositive
— wildlife-farm upstream investigations were never completed and China has not shared animal-source
data — but it is the principal empirical deficit of the market hypothesis (WHO) .

The lab case carries the mirror-image hole: no laboratory anywhere is known to have possessed SARS-CoV-
2 or a suﬀiciently close progenitor before the outbreak; the ODNI reported in June 2023 “no indications
that WIV research involved SARS-CoV-2 or a close progenitor virus,” and the intelligence community
judges (low confidence) the virus was probably not genetically engineered (FactCheck.org) . That ab-
sence, too, is qualified by non-cooperation: the WIV’s own database of ~22,000 samples and sequences
— including more than 100 unpublished bat-coronavirus sequences — went offline on 12 September 2019
and has never been shared, so “not found” and “not inspectable” are doing similar work on both sides
(The Washington Post) . A rigorous seed must therefore treat the two absences symmetrically: each
side’s strongest negative evidence is hostage to Chinese records that have never been produced.

3.6 Data-suppression episodes on the scientific record

Three episodes bear on the reliability of the scientific data itself, distinct from the political record.
First, in June 2021 Jesse Bloom (Fred Hutchinson) recovered 13 early SARS-CoV-2 sequences that
Wuhan University researchers had posted to the NIH Sequence Read Archive in March 2020 and then
requested be deleted in June 2020; Bloom wrote that it “seems likely that the sequences were deleted to
obscure their existence” (Science | AAAS) . The mundane explanation — the journal Small dropped the
data-availability statement in copy-editing, and the authors withdrew the reads to avoid version-control
problems before reposting to a Chinese database — was later corroborated by Wiley’s integrity team,
and a 2024 re-examination concluded the samples were indeed collected on or after 30 January 2020, as
the Chinese authors said (bioRxiv) . The recovered sequences nonetheless mattered scientifically: they
lacked three mutations seen in market-linked viruses, supporting early circulation outside the market
(Science | AAAS) .

Second, the WIV’s main coronavirus database — the most important bat-coronavirus database in China,
holding records of ~22,000 samples, over 100 unpublished sequences, and the full sequences of eight
viruses sampled in 2015 from the Mojiang region — became unreachable from outside the institute on
12 September 2019; Shi Zhengli says it was taken offline for security reasons after hacking attempts
(The Washington Post) . Third, raw market environmental-sequence data were briefly posted to GISAID
by Chinese CDC scientists in early 2023, enabling the Crits-Christoph analysis, then removed — the
episode that prompted WHO to call China’s non-disclosure “inexcusable” (Science | AAAS) . Whatever
the motive in each case, the pattern is that the open scientific record on both the animal and laboratory
sides is incomplete specifically where Chinese custodianship is involved, a point SAGO formalized: China
has not shared hundreds of early-patient sequences, detailed animal-source information, or laboratory
biosafety documentation (WHO) .




4. The laboratory side: the Wuhan research program
4.1 The WIV–EcoHealth–NIH funding channel

The Wuhan Institute of Virology is China’s foremost SARS-coronavirus laboratory and sits roughly
eight miles from the Huanan market; it held one of the world’s largest collections of bat-coronavirus
samples (New York Magazine) . Between 2014 and 2019, the US NIH funded EcoHealth Alliance’s
“Understanding the Risk of Bat Coronavirus Emergence” project, under which subawards flowed to the
WIV — Fauci, in his June 2024 testimony, characterized the Wuhan subaward as “a $120,000 a year
grant in a $6 billion budget” (InfluenceWatch) . In 2015, Ralph Baric (UNC) and Shi Zhengli (WIV)
published NIAID-supported work combining a bat-coronavirus spike with a SARS backbone that could
infect human airway cells — the experiment Rand Paul recited at the July 2026 hearing as “gained
transmissibility. It gained function.” (Rev)

Whether this work met the regulatory definition of gain-of-function became a years-long semantic war.
Fauci’s consistent position, restated under oath in 2024, is that he used the “operative definition” under
the HHS P3CO framework and that, on phylogenetics, “the viruses that were funded by the NIH … could
not be the precursor of SARS-CoV-2” — a narrow claim that is scientifically well supported but answers
a different question than the one about unfunded or undisclosed work at the WIV, as Congressman
Griﬀith’s exchange with Fauci (“maybe they used some of our money to get started, maybe they didn’t…
a group of scientists getting together might very well at Wuhan have said, ‘Hey, let’s see what happens
if we go over here and do this’ ”) made explicit, with Fauci conceding he “cannot account … for other

things that might be going on in China” (house.gov) . The NIH’s own oversight record was later judged

— findings the House cited and HHS later used as grounds for debarment (Medpage Today) .

4.2 DEFUSE: the 2018 proposal at the center of the circumstantial case

In March 2018, EcoHealth Alliance (Peter Daszak, PI), with UNC (Baric), the WIV (Shi Zhengli),
Duke-NUS and others, submitted Project DEFUSE to DARPA’s PREEMPT program, requesting
$14,209,245 for work in Kunming, Wuhan, Chapel Hill, New York, Singapore and elsewhere (archive.org)
. The proposal described creating full-length infectious clones of bat SARS-related coronaviruses and
inserting proteolytic cleavage sites — “of particular interest … a type of cleavage site able to interact with
furin” — into bat coronavirus spikes (The Intercept) . DARPA rejected it. When SARS-CoV-2 emerged
twenty months later in the partner city, bearing exactly such a furin cleavage site, DEFUSE became the
central documentary exhibit of the lab-leak case: as Paul put it at the July 2026 hearing, “this was either
an extraordinary coincidence or a smoking gun” (Rev) .

The details that keep DEFUSE load-bearing are in the drafts. Margin comments released by U.S. Right
to Know show Daszak telling Baric and Shi: “Once we get the funds, we can then allocate who does what
exact work, and I believe that a lot of these assays can be done in Wuhan as well,” and an early draft
specifying BSL-2 as “highly cost-effective” — to which Baric objected: “In the US, these recombinant
SARS CoV are studied under BSL3, not BSL2 … In china, might be growin these virus under bsl2. US
researchers will likely freak out” (Vanity Fair) . At least fifteen federal agencies attended the DARPA
PREEMPT Proposers Day where DEFUSE was pitched, yet the proposal’s existence was not public until
2021 (senate.gov) . And in April 2026 testimony released by Senator Paul in July, Baric told the Senate
that the DEFUSE design “could have resulted in the creation of SARS-CoV-2,” that Shi could have
performed the work alone without US involvement (“Research grants are always written in the context of
being more fluid than contracts”), and that DARPA itself had solicited the cross-species-jumping research
in January 2018 — though Baric also said he still believes an animal origin is “most likely” (Washington
Examiner) . Newly obtained NIH records further indicate the DEFUSE concepts — spike swaps, receptor-
binding changes, cleavage-site insertions — were embedded in multiple US-funded coronavirus projects
years before the pandemic, making DEFUSE less an isolated bid than a visible specimen of an ongoing
research program (U.S. Right To Know) .

4.3 Biosafety at the WIV

The declassified record on WIV biosafety is unusually specific. The ODNI’s June 2023 report states
that as of January 2019, WIV researchers performed SARS-like coronavirus experiments in
BSL-2 laboratories, “despite acknowledgements going back to 2017 of these viruses’ ability to directly
infect humans through their spike protein and early 2019 warnings of the danger of this practice”; that
some WIV researchers “probably did not use adequate biosafety precautions at least some of the time”;
that a 2017 WIV dissertation demonstrated reverse-genetic cloning of SARS-like coronaviruses leaving
no traces of genetic modification; that the WIV planned live-virus isolation from pangolin samples
in fall 2019; and that a 2020 inspection found aging equipment, insuﬀicient disinfectant capacity and
ventilation problems (Homeland Security Digital Library) . Baric testified in January 2024 that he had
personally warned Shi Zhengli to move the work to BSL-3 and she did not; he called the claim that WIV
coronavirus work had adequate biosafety “a load of BS,” and recounted discovering that his humanized
mice — shared under a use-restricted agreement — were being offered for sale by a Chinese commercial
company (Vanity Fair) .

4.4 Sick researchers and the offline database, autumn 2019

The State Department’s January 2021 fact sheet asserted that the US government “has reason to believe
that several researchers inside the WIV became sick in autumn 2019, before the first identified case
of the outbreak, with symptoms consistent with both COVID-19 and common seasonal illnesses” (The
Washington Post) . The ODNI’s June 2023 report deflated the sharpest version of this: several WIV
researchers were indeed ill in fall 2019, but with “a range of symptoms consistent with colds or allergies,”
some confirmed to have had unrelated illnesses, none known to have been hospitalized for COVID-like
symptoms (one may have been hospitalized for a non-respiratory condition), and the IC “is unable to
confirm if any of them handled live viruses” — concluding the episode “neither supports nor refutes either
hypothesis” (Homeland Security Digital Library) . The September 2019 database takedown is firmer as a
fact and softer as an inference: it happened on 12 September 2019, three months before the outbreak, it
removed ~22,000 sample records from outside view, and the offered explanation — hacking — has never
been verifiable (The Washington Post) . Neither item is proof; together with the biosafety record they
form the “opportunity” leg of the circumstantial case.

4.5 The Mojiang miners and RaTG13

The deepest backstory link between the WIV and severe pneumonia predates the pandemic by seven
years. In April 2012, six miners clearing bat feces from the Tongguan mineshaft in Mojiang, Yunnan,
developed severe atypical pneumonia; three died; a Kunming medical master’s thesis concluded a SARS-
like coronavirus from horseshoe bats was the likely agent, and prominent pulmonologist Zhong Nanshan
consulted remotely (Frontiers) . The WIV sampled the mine repeatedly between 2012 and 2015, isolating
293 coronaviruses — among them, in 2013, the sample later renamed RaTG13, the closest known relative
of SARS-CoV-2 (wikipedia.org) . Shi Zhengli attributed the miners’ deaths to fungus in a 2020 Scientific
American interview, said the miners’ serum (sent to WIV) tested negative for SARSr-CoV antibodies,
and did not disclose the mine–RaTG13 link in print until July 2020 (Frontiers) .

What Mojiang proves is contested in both directions. For the lab side, it shows the WIV surveilling a
site where a SARS-like virus plausibly killed humans, holding unpublished sequences from it, and being
less than forthright about the connection (Frontiers) . For the zoonotic side, the miners’ illness is equally
a demonstration that bat-coronavirus spillover into humans happens in nature without any laboratory —
the ecological prior on which the market case rests — and WIV retesting of miners’ serum in 2020 for
SARS-CoV-2 was negative (wikipedia.org) . The mine’s eight unpublished 2015 virus sequences sit in
the offline database; they are one of the concrete data items whose release would bear directly on the
progenitor question (The Washington Post) .

5.1 The 1 February 2020 teleconference and the making of “Proximal Origin”

On 31 January 2020, Kristian Andersen emailed Fauci that “the unusual features of the virus make up
a really small part of the genome (<0.1%) so one has to look really closely at all the sequences to see
that some of the features (potentially) look engineered,” adding that he, Eddie Holmes, Bob Garry and
Mike Farzan “all find the genome inconsistent with expectations from evolutionary theory” (USA Today)
. The next day, Jeremy Farrar convened a confidential teleconference — Fauci, Francis Collins, Andersen,
Holmes, Garry, Andrew Rambaut, Ron Fouchier, Patrick Vallance and others — which participants
agreed to treat “in total confidence”; that morning Fauci emailed his deputy Hugh Auchincloss: “It is
essential that we speak this AM … You will have tasks today that must be done,” later explaining he
wanted to know “the scope of what our collaborations were and the kind of work that we were funding in
China” (The Nation) . On the call, per later testimony, Farzan put lab origin at 70/30, Farrar at 50-50,
and Holmes at 60-40 leaning lab (U.S. Right To Know) .

Within four days the group’s public posture inverted. On 4 February, Andersen told the National
Academies drafting group that “the data conclusively show” no engineering, calling such theories “crack-
pot” — while specifying to Congress later that he meant engineered with intent, and that he personally
kept a lab-culture possibility open until mid-to-late February (USA Today) . On 4 February Farrar sent
Fauci and Collins a “very rough first draft from Eddie and team”; on 8 February Andersen wrote that
“our main work over the past couple of weeks has been to disprove any type of lab theory, but we are at
a crossroad where the scientific evidence isn’t conclusive enough”; on 12 February he pitched the paper to
Nature as rebutting “speculation, fear mongering, and conspiracies,” invoking Farrar, Fauci and Collins
by name; and on 17 February, minutes before the preprint posted, Farrar requested the key sentence be
softened from “unlikely” to “improbable” that SARS-CoV-2 emerged through laboratory manipulation
(U.S. Right To Know) . The paper — “The Proximal Origin of SARS-CoV-2,” Nature Medicine, 17
March 2020 — concluded “we do not believe that any type of laboratory-based scenario is plausible,” has
been accessed more than 5.6 million times, and became the load-bearing citation for dismissing the lab
hypothesis (The Intercept) .

5.2 The Lancet letter and the suppression claim

On 19 February 2020, The Lancet published a letter from 27 scientists “strongly condemn[ing] conspiracy
theories suggesting that COVID-19 does not have a natural origin,” with all signatories declaring no
competing interests (wikipedia.org) . FOIA emails later showed the letter was organized by Daszak,
who deliberately omitted both EcoHealth’s WIV partnership and Baric’s name to feign impartiality; The
Lancet added a competing-interests addendum in June 2021 after sustained criticism, and editor Richard
Horton conceded Daszak “should certainly have declared his competing interests right at the beginning”
(LibGuides at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center) . The letter’s effect on discourse was substantial:
Vanity Fair’s Katherine Eban described its “chilling effect,” and critics including Nicholas Wade noted
the signatories were “assuring the public of facts they could not know for sure were true” (wikipedia.org)
.

5.3 Morens and the FOIA-evasion record (May 2024)

The May 2024 hearing of Dr. David Morens — Fauci’s senior scientific adviser for decades — produced the
investigation’s most damaging primary documents. Morens wrote on 24 February 2021: “i learned from
our foia lady here how to make emails disappear after i am foia’d but before the search starts, so i think we
are all safe. Plus i deleted most of those earlier emails after sending them to gmail” (kffhealthnews.org) .
Under questioning he admitted using personal email for oﬀicial business to avoid FOIA (“I shouldn’t have
done that. That’s wrong”), was confronted with evidence of intentional misspellings to defeat keyword
searches and of backchannel routes to Fauci, and was told by Rep. McCormick that, given conflicting
statements, he had lied either in his transcribed interview or at the hearing (house.gov) . Both parties
condemned the conduct; Chairman Wenstrup wrote to NIH that the evidence “suggests a conspiracy
at the highest levels of NIH and NIAID to avoid public transparency,” while Democrats stressed the
documents implicate records practices, not the virus (kffhealthnews.org) .

Morens’s emails also show him backchanneling confidential NIH grant information to Daszak — his
“best-friend” — while EcoHealth’s grant was under review (house.gov) . The hearing’s significance for
the origins case is indirect but real: it demonstrated that senior NIAID staff handled origins-related
correspondence through channels designed to be invisible to FOIA and to Congress, which means the
public documentary record of early-2020 deliberations was, by design, incomplete. Congressman Griﬀith
drew the civil-law analogy explicitly at the June 2024 Fauci hearing: “when one party has destroyed
or refuses to produce evidence … a jury is allowed to draw an adverse inference that the information
destroyed or not produced was unfavorable” (Rev) . Whether that inference is fair is contested; that the
deletions occurred is not (house.gov) .

5.4 Fauci’s transcribed interview (January 2024) and public hearing (June 2024)

Across a two-day, 14-hour transcribed interview in January 2024, Fauci made several concessions the
subcommittee later weaponized and Democrats later contextualized: the six-foot distancing rule “sort
of just appeared” without supporting trials; vaccine mandates may have increased hesitancy; he said
“I do not recall” more than 100 times; and — the headline — the lab-leak hypothesis “in and of
itself isn’t inherently a conspiracy theory,” a marked retreat from the 2020–2021 discourse his own
circle had shaped (house.gov) . On gain-of-function he insisted on the P3CO “operative definition,” the
semantic position that underlay his 2021 Senate denials that NIH funded gain-of-function research in
Wuhan (house.gov) .

5.5 The Select Subcommittee final report (December 2024) and the minority response

On 2 December 2024, the Republican majority released its 520-page final report after 25 hearings, 30+
transcribed interviews and one million pages of documents, concluding COVID-19 “most likely emerged
from a laboratory in Wuhan” on five argued grounds: the FCS “not found in nature”; all cases from “a
single introduction”; Wuhan’s SARS lab working at “inadequate biosafety levels”; sick WIV researchers in
fall 2019; and the non-appearance of natural-origin evidence (house.gov) . The report also concluded Fauci
“prompted” Proximal Origin “to push the preferred narrative,” that EcoHealth “facilitated dangerous gain-
of-function research … and misled the NIH,” and — newly revealed — that the Department of Justice
had empaneled a grand jury connected to COVID-19 origins, subpoenaing EcoHealth documents, an
investigation whose details and status remain unknown (The Hill) .

Two cautions are due on this report. It contains no new direct evidence of a lab leak — Science’s
characterization — and its five signature claims are contested by the scientific record: the FCS does
exist in other coronavirus lineages, and the two-lineage analysis argues against “a single introduction”
(Science | AAAS) . The Democratic minority report concluded “a zoonotic origin and lab accident are
both plausible, as is a ‘hybrid’ scenario,” defended Fauci, and noted that WIV viruses studied with
EcoHealth funding were too distantly related to be the progenitor (The BMJ) . Fact-checkers’ verdict on
the five-point formulation (later reused verbatim by the White House webpage, Section 6.2): each point
is “incorrect or misleading” in its strong form, even though the underlying circumstantial questions are
legitimate (FactCheck.org) .

5.6 Debarment, the pardon, and legal jeopardy

On 17 January 2025 — the Biden administration’s final days — HHS formally debarred EcoHealth
Alliance and Peter Daszak for five years, citing the subcommittee-uncovered record: ignored over-
sight requests, failure to report the humanized-mouse experiment, and a progress report two years late;
EcoHealth had terminated Daszak effective 6 January 2025 (house.gov) . Senator Kennedy characterized
the cited experiments as modifying bat coronaviruses “to make them 10,000 times more infectious in
mice” (U.S. Senator John Kennedy) . On 20 January 2025, President Biden issued Fauci a preemptive
pardon covering any federal offenses from 1 January 2014 through 19 January 2025 — a shield with
holes, as later legal analysis noted: it does not cover perjury after 19 January 2025 or state offenses, and
three state attorneys general subsequently subpoenaed Fauci (The Hill) .

The legal posture matters to the case only as context for 2026: it is why, when Rand Paul subpoenaed
him, Fauci’s lawyers judged that any sworn answer could seed a perjury theory despite the pardon, and
why the contempt fight turned on whether a pardoned witness retains Fifth Amendment protection —
with former prosecutors split, and Democrats warning the referral “will create a damaging precedent” for
oversight generally (The Hill) . Whatever one’s view of the merits, the sequence — debarment of the

5.7 The June 2026 ODNI “Fauci Files”

On 18–19 June 2026, in one of her final acts as Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard released 67
documents across 391 pages, headlined “Fauci Funded Wuhan Lab Research That Sparked COVID,”
alleging Fauci lied to Congress under oath in 2024, manipulated intelligence assessments, and ran a “deep
state playbook” (WBMA) . The genuine news value in the drop is that it documents Fauci’s participation
in a February 2020 expert meeting on data needs and a June 2021 IC briefing in which he discussed sick
WIV researchers and recommended scientists to consult (lawfaremedia.org) .

Lawfare’s document-level review is the essential counterweight and should be cited alongside the release
itself: the files contain no intelligence assessment concluding Fauci-funded research “sparked COVID”;
the CIA’s internal email states it has “no record of Dr. Fauci coming to CIA headquarters” to
discuss origins (contradicting Paul’s implication); IC oﬀicials considered Fauci as an outside reviewer
and rejected him as conflicted; after a contentious House briefing an ODNI oﬀicial wrote it was
“laughable” to claim lab-leaning experts were excluded — “every expert they named was consulted”; and
the “analysts were paid off” whistleblower claim is paired in the file with its alternative explanation
(late-arriving information) (lawfaremedia.org) . Independent fact-checkers reached the same bottom line:
across ~400 pages there is no definitive link proving Fauci knowingly concealed an origin, and no new
evidence for either hypothesis (WBMA) . The drop is thus strongest evidence for a meta claim — that
origins intelligence work and political actors were entangled — and weak evidence for any origin claim.

5.8 The Proximal Origin Slack messages (22 July 2026)

On 22 July 2026, Paul released internal Slack messages among the Proximal Origin authors showing that,
three months after publishing that no laboratory scenario was plausible, Andersen assigned a 30%
probability to a lab origin, Holmes 20% (later revised to 10%); Andersen wrote “our paper
was pretty strong in saying ‘there’s no way,’ but I have less confidence in that statement at this stage,”
and reacted to German researchers building a SARS-CoV-2 reverse-genetics system in under two weeks
with “Clearly it’s not that hard” (senate.gov) . The messages also show the group coordinating with
the intelligence community during its 90-day review (Andersen expecting to “have a handler”), Holmes
repeatedly citing a Chinese source he called “my spy,” and — after DEFUSE leaked — discussing the need
to “stay off email,” to “carefully curate” correspondence “for the FOI records,” and to “get something
back to Tony [Fauci]’s team at NIH” (senate.gov) .

Interpretation splits predictably and both readings should be preserved. To investigators, this is the sup-
pression claim confirmed in the authors’ own words: public certainty outrunning private belief, records
curated against FOIA, and an NIH feedback loop (senate.gov) . To the authors’ defenders, private prob-
ability estimates of 10–30% are what scientific uncertainty looks like; the paper’s conclusion concerned
deliberate engineering (“not a laboratory construct or a purposefully manipulated virus”), which the
Slack messages did not resurrect; and holding a minority-probability scenario while publishing the best-
supported one is normal science, not concealment (The Conversation) . What the messages indisputably
change is the status of the paper as a consensus marker: the document most often cited as “the science
has spoken” was written by people who, in their own contemporaneous words, had not finished speaking
(senate.gov) .

6.2 The politicization layer (2025–2026)

On 18 April 2025 the White House redirected covid.gov to a page titled “Lab Leak: The True Origins
of COVID-19,” presenting the subcommittee’s five points and accusing Fauci of pushing a “preferred
narrative”; virologist Angela Rasmussen responded that “every one of the five pieces of evidence … is
factually incorrect, embellished, or presented in a misleading way,” and FactCheck.org documented that
the administration’s claim of a “confirmed” lab origin misstates the agencies’ low/moderate-confidence
language (ABC News) . China answered on 30 April 2025 with a State Council white paper claiming
the virus may have circulated in the US earlier — citing July 2019 Virginia pneumonia reports, Florida
data, 28 UNC lab incidents, and the 2019 Fort Detrick shutdown — and declaring China’s own investi-
gation complete (BBC Monitoring) . A Missouri federal court’s 7 March 2025 default judgment ordering
China to pay $24.49 billion added a judicial-political thread Beijing rejects outright (The State Council
Information Oﬀice of China) .

For the case file, the politicization layer matters because it changed what “oﬀicial position” means: since
January 2025, the US government’s political organs assert lab origin as settled while its intelligence organs
hold it at low-to-moderate confidence and its scientific literature leans the other way. Any honest claim
ladder must therefore keep these three evidentiary systems separate rather than averaging them (NBC
News) .

8. Where the disagreement lives
Crux 1 — epicenter vs. origin. Both sides now accept the market was the early epicenter; the
dispute is whether the market was the spillover site (zoonosis) or a superspreading node seeded by an
infected person (lab incident). The evidence distinguishing these is thin because the market was cleared
and disinfected before animal sampling, and the animal-source farms were never investigated (Science |
AAAS) . This crux is resolvable in principle: wildlife-farm traceback data and the animals’ supply-chain
records exist in China and have never been shared (Middle East Health) .

Crux 2 — one introduction or two. The two-lineage result currently does the most work for zoonosis
(SAGO treats it as a signature “rather than … a single virus introduction”), and it is also the result most
sensitive to data withheld from it — rooting, missing early genomes, the 500+ unpublished sequences
(World Health Organization (WHO)) . If released early genomes collapse A and B into one introduction,


                                                                                       Generated by Kimi
the zoonotic case loses its strongest quantitative pillar; if they reinforce two market-linked introductions,
the lab case loses its “single introduction” argument.

Crux 3 — absence of evidence on both sides. No infected intermediate host after ~80,000 animals;
no progenitor in any declared collection. Each side reads the other’s absence as telling and its own as
merely hard (Science | AAAS) . The asymmetry that can break the tie is custodianship: the lab side’s
absence is potentially remediable by WIV records (database, notebooks, health files); the animal side’s
absence is remediable by farm/trade records. Both sets are in Chinese hands, which is why SAGO’s
report reads simultaneously as a zoonosis-leaning assessment and an indictment of the data environment
(WHO) .

Crux 4 — the FCS as signal or noise. The genome cannot adjudicate itself: identical features (unique-
among-sarbecoviruses FCS, rare CGG-CGG, suboptimal motif) are read as anomalous by one side and
as within natural variation by the other, with serious scientists on both sides (Baltimore’s “smoking
gun” remark and partial walkback; Weiss/Perlman’s suboptimality argument) (California Institute of
Technology) . This crux would yield to phylogenetic discovery — a natural sarbecovirus with an FCS —
or to documentary discovery of FCS-insertion work at WIV, not to further argument about the sequence
alone (IntechOpen) .

Crux 5 — what the conduct record proves. The hearings documented real misconduct (records
evasion, conflict-concealed letters, public-private divergence) (house.gov) . The unresolved interpretive
question is whether that conduct was (a) evidence-conscious concealment of a feared lab origin, or (b)
ordinary institutional self-protection under unprecedented scrutiny — and, critically, whether either
answer changes the virological evidence. The seed should keep this crux explicitly orthogonal: misconduct
can justify re-weighing testimony without constituting origin evidence.

is diplomatic, and their current probability is low given that China has declared origins-tracing “finished”

10. Load-bearing claims and weakest links
Load-bearing for zoonosis: COV-C001 (epicenter clustering — the sturdiest item in the whole case),
COV-C002 (two introductions), COV-C003 (wildlife co-location). If C002 falls to released data, the
zoonotic case retreats to “market amplification + unknown upstream event,” materially weakening it
against the lab scenario (Science | AAAS) .

Load-bearing for a research-related origin: COV-C007 (DEFUSE’s existence and content), COV-
C008 (BSL-2 practice), COV-C005 (unverifiability of lab holdings). The lab case currently has no direct
positive evidence — no progenitor, no incident, no whistleblower; its strength is the conjunction of
opportunity, capability, intent-on-paper, and opacity (archive.org) .

Weakest links on each side: For zoonosis — COV-C004 (the missing host; six years, ~80,000 animals,
zero positives) and the dependence of market metagenomics on post-clearance surface swabs (Science |
AAAS) . For the lab case — the sick-researchers item (COV-C010), which the ODNI explicitly says “nei-
ther supports nor refutes either hypothesis,” and the FCS-uniqueness argument in its strong “not found
in nature” form, which is factually wrong as stated on the White House page (Homeland Security Digital
Library) . For the meta-claims — the suppression narrative’s strong version (COV-C016, intelligence ma-
nipulation) is currently contradicted by the very documents released to support it, while its moderate
version (COV-C012, public certainty exceeding private belief) is supported (lawfaremedia.org) .

11. The epistemic environment
Public belief has been decoupled from the specialist literature for years: 64–66% of Americans leaned
lab-leak in March 2023 polling while the two flagship Science papers had moved specialists toward the
market; a 2021 PRRI survey found 39% of Americans believed the virus was “intentionally developed
in a laboratory,” rising to 85% among QAnon believers — figures sociologists cite in mapping how the
Fauci-centered conspiracy frame absorbed the legitimate lab-accident question (The Washington Post)
. The Conversation’s analysis of the July 2026 hearing draws the line the atlas should keep bright:
“asking whether SARS-CoV-2 emerged through either zoonotic spillover or a laboratory-related incident
is not inherently a conspiracy theory,” but the transformation of “absence of evidence into evidence of
concealment” is the signature move by which the question becomes one (The Conversation) .

The information environment degraded further from both directions in 2025–2026: a US government
webpage asserting certainty the intelligence community does not hold, and a Chinese white paper reviving
Fort Detrick insinuations while refusing the specific data requests (early sequences, animal sources, lab
records) that would actually resolve the case (FactCheck.org) . Meanwhile the mortality ledger — roughly
14.8 million excess deaths across 2020–2021 by WHO estimate — is the standing argument for why the
open questions in Sections 8–9 are worth what they cost (nih.gov) . The case is not closed; it is, in the
literal sense, unexamined where it counts.
