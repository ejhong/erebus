## 1. Scope recommendation for a first cut

### 1.1 The case question

Take brief 2's formulation, which is sharper than "did COVID come from a lab":

> **What event initiated sustained human transmission of SARS-CoV-2 in Wuhan in late
> 2019?**

This separates SARS-CoV-2 (the virus) from COVID-19 (the disease), and it lets the
hypotheses be genuinely distinct rather than a featured thesis plus its negation.

### 1.2 What is in scope for the first cut

Eighteen featured claims across six themes. Everything else goes to catalog tier or
waits for a second pass.

| Theme (suggested key) | Featured claims | What it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `early-geography` | 4 | Worobey 2022 early-case geography and its critiques |
| `market-animals` | 4 | Liu 2023 environmental sampling, Crits-Christoph 2024 metagenomics, the missing animal |
| `lineages` | 2 | Pekar 2022 double-introduction argument and its contested status |
| `genome` | 3 | Furin cleavage site, engineering-signature question, closest known relatives |
| `wiv-program` | 5 | DEFUSE, BSL-2 practice, database offline, autumn-2019 illnesses, Mojiang/RaTG13 |
| `assessments` | 2 | The IC split and its confidence language; SAGO 2025 |

Plus **two catalog-tier institutional-integrity claims** (HHS OIG grant oversight;
records-concealment conduct) held deliberately *off* the origin ladder — see §1.4.

### 1.3 What is deliberately out of scope for the first cut

Cutting these is what makes the case manageable. Each is legitimate; none is load-bearing
for the origin question, and several are living-persons minefields with low evidential
yield.

- **The Fauci accountability thread as a whole** — the January 2024 transcribed
  interview, the June 2024 public hearing, the January 2025 pardon, the July 2026 Fifth
  Amendment invocation, the August 2026 contempt referral. Per both briefs this produces
  **no origin evidence**. Brief 2 is explicit: "a neutral evidence system should not
  assign the invocation a positive laboratory-origin score." A short "what this case
  does not grade" note on the case page is the right treatment.
- **The Lancet letter (Feb 2020) and its competing-interests addendum.** Real conduct
  record; zero diagnosticity between hypotheses.
- **The gain-of-function definitional dispute (P3CO / ePPP).** Semantic, and both briefs
  agree the funded viruses are phylogenetically excluded as precursors — so the dispute
  cannot move the origin question.
- **The deleted-SRA-sequences episode (Bloom 2021).** Brief 1 reports the mundane
  explanation was corroborated by Wiley's integrity team and a 2024 re-examination.
  Interesting, low yield; catalog tier at most.
- **The politicization layer** (April 2025 White House covid.gov page; April 2025 China
  State Council white paper; the Missouri default judgment; polling divergence).
  Context for the overview article, not claims.
- **The June 2026 ODNI release, the May 2026 CIA-officer testimony, and the Fauci diary.**
  All three are documentary corpora whose *contents* have not been analyzed in either
  brief beyond press characterizations. Brief 1 reports that Lawfare's document-level
  review found the release "contains no intelligence assessment concluding Fauci-funded
  research 'sparked COVID'." Park these as research opportunities, not claims.
- **Cold-chain importation.** Brief 2 scores it "weakly supported as the initiating Wuhan
  pathway." One sentence in the hypothesis set; no claim.
- **Deliberate release / bioweapon.** Named in the hypothesis set as non-serious with the
  reason given, and not graded further.

### 1.4 The three-layer separation (adopt this)

Brief 2's single most valuable structural recommendation, and the discipline that keeps
this case honest:

1. **Biological-origin layer** — what infected the first humans.
2. **Research-capability layer** — what laboratories could do or planned to do.
3. **Institutional-integrity layer** — records handling, conflicts, grant oversight,
   candor to investigators.

Layers 2 and 3 must not silently feed the layer-1 verdict. Misconduct in layer 3 licenses
re-weighting *testimony*; it is not origin evidence. Conversely, strong layer-1 market
evidence does not retire layer-3 questions. On the case page this should be visible
structure — separate component verdicts, not one merged meter.

## 2. Case framing (draft text)

Draft prose for `case.yaml` fields. Every factual assertion inside traces to a brief and
must be verified before it ships.

### `whatIsClaimed`

> Two families of explanation compete. The zoonotic family holds that SARS-CoV-2 reached
> humans from wildlife — at or immediately upstream of the Huanan Seafood Wholesale
> Market, most likely through the live-animal trade, in roughly November–December 2019.
> The research-related family holds that the virus, or a close progenitor, was present in
> a Wuhan research program — most plausibly the Wuhan Institute of Virology — and reached
> the city through an infected person: a field sampler, a laboratory worker handling a
> naturally collected virus, or a worker exposed to a passaged or engineered construct.
> Under both families the market matters; they disagree about whether it was where the
> virus entered humans or where an already-human virus amplified. Two further claims sit
> underneath and are contested on their own terms: whether the early scientific
> "consensus" for zoonosis was reached by ordinary data-driven deliberation, and whether
> officials and funded scientists steered the early narrative. This case grades the
> evidence disputes. It does not grade anyone's culpability.

### `whereDisagreementLives`

> Not over whether the market was the early epicenter — the geographic concentration of
> the earliest known cases around Huanan is the sturdiest finding in the case, and both
> sides now largely accept it. The live disputes sit one rung up. First, epicenter versus
> origin: the market was closed, cleared, and disinfected before systematic sampling
> began, so environmental RNA cannot say whether an infected animal, vendor, or customer
> deposited it. Second, one introduction or two: the double-spillover inference is
> model-dependent and rests on early sequence data that is partly unpublished. Third, the
> two symmetrical absences — no infected intermediate animal after years of sampling, and
> no laboratory anywhere known to have held SARS-CoV-2 or a close progenitor. Each side
> reads the other's absence as telling and its own as merely hard. What makes the
> asymmetry decidable in principle rather than in practice is custodianship: the records
> that would resolve either absence — early patient sequences, wildlife supply-chain and
> farm data, laboratory inventories, worker health files — are held in China and have not
> been produced.

### `whatWouldSettleIt`

> Specific documents and datasets, not further argument. On the zoonotic side: original
> trader and farm records for the market's western-section wildlife vendors, archived 2019
> animal tissues and sera, and the raw market metagenomic reads with complete stall
> metadata — a pre-outbreak animal carrying SARS-CoV-2 or a phylogenetically basal
> near-progenitor would be direct evidence of the transmission chain. On the
> research-related side: the Wuhan Institute of Virology's offline sample database with
> raw sequence files and freezer inventories, and de-identified 2019 occupational-health
> records with stored sera — a timestamped pre-pandemic virus above roughly 99% identity,
> or a verified 2019 seroconversion, would transform the hypothesis; a complete,
> independently audited inventory lacking one would substantially weaken it. Common to
> both: the several hundred early-patient sequences WHO says remain unavailable, and a
> preregistered adversarial reanalysis of the early-lineage question with proponents and
> critics fixing methods before unblinding. Most of the first group are acts of political
> will rather than research programs; the last is fundable at analysis scale and is the
> single best value for money in the case.

### `bestConventionalExplanation` (steelmanned zoonosis)

> A sarbecovirus circulating in bats, plausibly via one or more intermediate mammal hosts,
> entered the live-wildlife supply chain and reached Wuhan, where it crossed into humans
> at or immediately upstream of the Huanan market in roughly November–December 2019 —
> most likely more than once. This is not an argument from the absence of a lab smoking
> gun; it is the only hypothesis with multiple independent affirmative lines of public
> evidence, and they converge on the same few hundred square metres. The earliest known
> cases cluster around the market, and — the observation that carries the statistical
> weight — cases with *no* reported market link lived closer to it than cases who worked
> there, which is what exposure at the market predicts and what contact-tracing artifact
> does not. Excess pneumonia deaths rose first in the surrounding districts, and later
> seroprevalence was highest there. Inside the market, SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental
> samples concentrate in the western section where live-mammal vendors operated;
> mitochondrial DNA from experimentally susceptible species — raccoon dog, masked palm
> civet, hoary bamboo rat — co-occurs with viral RNA in samples from a wildlife stall; and
> raccoon dogs and civets have documented history as SARS-CoV-1 amplifiers. The earliest
> viral diversity partitions into two lineages whose joint pattern is better explained by
> repeated introduction than by a single seeding event, which is easy under wildlife-trade
> spillover and awkward under one infected worker. The genome carries no accepted forensic
> signature of engineering; its most-discussed feature is explicable by recombination, and
> the naturally sampled relative pool (RaTG13, BANAL-52) demonstrates both that
> SARS-CoV-2-like receptor binding evolves in wildlife and that natural sarbecovirus
> diversity is badly undersampled. Wildlife-trade spillover is, additionally, the base-rate
> mechanism: it produced SARS-CoV-1 in the same country through the same trade two decades
> earlier, and the Mojiang miners demonstrate bat-coronavirus illness in humans with no
> laboratory involved. Its unpaid debt is real and should not be minimized: no infected
> source animal has ever been found, the upstream farm investigation was never completed,
> and the market's animals were gone before anyone sampled them. But "the smoking-gun
> animal is missing" is an argument about what China permitted investigators to look for,
> not a positive case for any alternative.

## 3. Hypothesis set

Stated so a proponent of each would accept the wording. The right-hand columns are what
the briefs say discriminates — and the honest headline is that **most individual
observations are compatible with several hypotheses.** Discrimination comes from a small
number of items, not from the volume of evidence.

### H1 — Zoonotic spillover at or immediately upstream of Huanan market

- **Statement:** A sarbecovirus of wildlife origin, plausibly via an intermediate mammal host
in the live-animal trade, crossed into humans at the Huanan market or in its immediate
supply chain in roughly November–December 2019, possibly more than once.

- **Predicts:** early cases geographically centered on the market including cases with no
recognized market exposure; viral RNA concentrated where susceptible mammals were sold;
susceptible-animal genetic material co-located with viral RNA; possibly multiple basal
lineages; a findable progenitor in the supply chain if anyone sampled it in time; no
engineering signature.

- **Strongest evidence:** early-case geography (Worobey 2022); western-section positivity;
wildlife mtDNA co-location (Crits-Christoph 2024); two-lineage structure (Pekar 2022);
SARS-CoV-1 base rate.

- **Strongest problem:** no infected animal, ever, despite the largest such search on record;
market cleared before sampling; upstream farm traceback never completed.

### H2 — Zoonotic or human spillover elsewhere in Wuhan or the region, with Huanan as an amplifier

- **Statement:** The first human infection occurred somewhere other than the market — upstream
in the trade, elsewhere in the city, or in the wider region — and the market functioned as
an unusually efficient amplification site for a virus already in humans.

- **Predicts:** early cases lacking market exposure; heavy market contamination without an
infected market animal; no requirement that the earliest lineage appear at the market.

- **Strongest evidence:** the earliest case by symptom onset reportedly had no market link;
lineage-A genomes reportedly lack market contact; the market's physical characteristics
(traffic volume, resident vendor population, enclosed poorly ventilated wildlife section,
cold wet surfaces, an on-site mahjong hall) make it a strong amplification candidate
independent of spillover.

- **Strongest problem:** the tight geographic relationship of early cases to the market,
including unlinked cases, is exactly what this hypothesis has to explain away.

**Note:** H2 is the hinge hypothesis and the one most often skipped. It is compatible with
*either* a natural upstream spillover *or* a research-related index infection, which is
why it must be a first-class hypothesis rather than a footnote. Brief 2 scores it
"plausible, less directly supported than market-linked zoonosis."

### H3 — Research-related incident: field-collection or fieldwork infection

- **Statement:** A researcher was infected with a naturally circulating virus during wildlife
sampling or handling in the field, and carried it to Wuhan.

- **Predicts:** an occupational-exposure record; a field sample containing a near-progenitor;
a travel chronology linking a sampling trip to Wuhan in the right window; worker serology.

- **Strongest evidence:** the WIV ran an extensive bat-sampling program with documented field
activity, including repeated sampling at the Mojiang mine; the IC's own definition of a
"laboratory-associated incident" includes research-related sampling exposure.

- **Strongest problem:** no documented pre-outbreak infected sampler, no matching field virus,
and the market clustering remains unexplained without an additional transmission chain.

### H4 — Laboratory incident with an unmodified, naturally collected virus

- **Statement:** A naturally collected SARS-related coronavirus held, cultured, or handled in
a Wuhan laboratory infected a worker, who carried it into the city.

- **Predicts:** a pre-outbreak inventory entry or raw sequence for a virus above roughly 99%
identity; culture and exposure logs; worker infection evidence.

- **Strongest evidence:** WIV held one of the world's largest bat-coronavirus collections;
conducted SARS-like coronavirus experiments in BSL-2 as of January 2019 per the ODNI;
biosafety practice was reported inadequate at least some of the time; the sample database
went offline in September 2019 and has never been shared.

- **Strongest problem:** the IC reported no indication that WIV pre-pandemic holdings included
SARS-CoV-2 or a close progenitor, and no direct evidence of a specific causative incident.
This hypothesis requires an undisclosed virus whose existence is asserted from opacity.

### H5 — Laboratory incident with an engineered, chimeric, or serially passaged virus

- **Statement:** A construct produced by reverse genetics, chimera assembly, or laboratory
adaptation infected a worker and escaped accidentally.

- **Predicts:** construct maps, plasmid and synthesis orders, passage records, raw reads, an
identifiable precursor backbone, and — depending on method — an evolutionary trajectory
signature.

- **Strongest evidence:** the March 2018 DEFUSE proposal explicitly contemplated introducing
human-specific/furin cleavage sites into SARS-related coronavirus spikes with WIV as a
partner, twenty months before a virus with such a site emerged in the partner city; WIV
possessed seamless reverse-genetics capability that leaves no engineering scar; a 2017 WIV
dissertation reportedly demonstrated trace-free cloning.

- **Strongest problem:** almost all US IC agencies assessed the virus was not genetically
engineered and most that it was not laboratory-adapted; SAGO judged the genomic features
explicable by natural recombination; 23 former SAGO members argue the specific DEFUSE
backbones and spike components are too evolutionarily distant to be a plausible direct
route; and no precursor construct has ever been identified.

### H6 — Deliberate engineering and release (not treated as a serious hypothesis)

- **Statement:** The virus was deliberately created and intentionally released.

- **Why it is not graded:** both briefs report that all US IC agencies assessed SARS-CoV-2 was
not developed as a biological weapon, and that neither brief could identify any
affirmative public evidence. Brief 2 scores its public affirmative-evidence density at
zero. Brief 1 records that this variant is "rejected by the entire US intelligence
community and by virtually all scientists on both sides." It is named on the case page so
readers can see it was considered and why it fails, and to keep the research-related
hypotheses from inheriting its disrepute.

### H7 — Cold-chain importation (named, not graded)

Physically possible for transported virus, but it does not explain the virus's
evolutionary origin, and SAGO reportedly found no persuasive evidence it explains the
Wuhan emergence. One line in the overview.

### Theme: `early-geography`

**★ COV-C001 — Market as early epicenter**
- **Wording:** "The Huanan Seafood Wholesale Market was the geographic center of the earliest
documented cluster of SARS-CoV-2 infections in Wuhan in December 2019."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `well_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `moderate`
- **Why moderate, not high:** an epicenter is predicted by market-spillover, by upstream
spillover with market arrival, and by market amplification of a virus from elsewhere. It
discriminates against non-market origins collectively, not among them.
- **Sources:** Worobey et al., Science 2022 (+ erratum); Huang et al., Lancet 2020; WHO-China
joint study 2021; SAGO 2025.
- **Objection to record:** rests entirely on Chinese case ascertainment; the complete December
line list has never been released.

**★ COV-C002 — Unlinked cases lived closer than linked cases**
- **Wording:** "Among the earliest documented cases, those with no reported Huanan market
exposure resided significantly closer to the market than cases who worked there."
- **Rung:** observation (measurement) · *Credibility:* `provisionally_supported`
- **Diagnosticity:** `high`
- **Why high:** this is the single observation the briefs identify as hardest for
ascertainment-bias explanations, and therefore the load-bearing item for market-site
spillover as against market amplification.
- **Why only provisionally supported:** it is a spatial-statistical result on a case set of
disputed completeness; brief 1 records ascertainment bias as "the principal
counterargument," answered in the paper but not independently reproduced. The Rootclaim
material disputes the paper's underlying comparison-site analysis.
- **Sources:** Worobey et al., Science 2022 (+ erratum, DOI to verify); Rootclaim response
(critique of the 1,676-comparison-sites analysis).

**★ COV-C003 — Earliest onset case had no reported market link**
- **Wording:** "Huang et al. reported that the earliest identified case by symptom onset
(1 December 2019) had no epidemiological link to the Huanan market, and that no such link
was found between that patient and later cases."
- **Rung:** observation (what the source states) · *Credibility:* `well_supported` as to the
publication's statement · *Diagnosticity:* `moderate`
- **Note:** frame as what the paper states, not as established fact about the outbreak — the
case's date and market status have been contested elsewhere, and neither brief resolves
that. This is the cleanest example in the case of AGENTS.md §3.9 (source statement vs.
inference).
- **Sources:** Huang et al., Lancet 2020; WHO-China joint study 2021.

**COV-C004 — Huanan's prior plausibility as an early-cluster site**
- **Wording:** "The Huanan market's physical and social characteristics made it a
substantially more likely site for the first *recognized* infection cluster in Wuhan than
a population-share calculation implies."
- **Rung:** mechanism · *Credibility:* `weakly_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `high` if
established
- **Why it belongs:** it is the strongest form of the lab-side counter to COV-C001/C002 —
attacking the likelihood ratio rather than the observation. If sustained, the geographic
signal loses most of its diagnostic force. Currently advocacy-sourced (Rootclaim), not
peer-reviewed, and the cited comparison outbreaks are all descendants of the Wuhan
outbreak rather than independent spillovers.
- **Sources:** Rootclaim response, April 2024 (traffic ~10,000 visitors/day; >1,000 resident
tenants; enclosed low-ventilation wildlife section; cold wet surfaces; on-site mahjong
hall, with a page locator into a Washington Post document compilation); Worobey et al.
2022 (the analysis being disputed).

### Theme: `market-animals`

**★ COV-C005 — Post-closure environmental sampling detected the virus in the market**
- **Wording:** "Chinese CDC investigators collected 923 environmental samples at the Huanan
market beginning after its 1 January 2020 closure and detected SARS-CoV-2 in the market
environment."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `well_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `low`
- **Why low:** environmental RNA cannot identify who deposited it — infected vendor, customer,
animal, or contaminated surface. Both briefs are explicit on this.
- **Sources:** Liu et al., Nature 2023.

**★ COV-C006 — Positive samples concentrated where live mammals were sold**
- **Wording:** "SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples inside the Huanan market were
spatially concentrated in the western section, where live-mammal vendors operated."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `provisionally_supported` · *Diagnosticity:*
`moderate`
- **Objection to record:** the Rootclaim material asserts the same area was the market's least
ventilated section with no outside connection — a competing non-spillover explanation for
concentrated positivity. Record it as a stated objection, unverified.
- **Sources:** Worobey et al., Science 2022; Liu et al., Nature 2023; Rootclaim response.

**★ COV-C007 — Susceptible-wildlife genetic material co-occurs with viral RNA**
- **Wording:** "Mitochondrial DNA from mammals susceptible to SARS-CoV-2 — including raccoon
dog, masked palm civet, hoary bamboo rat, and Malayan porcupine — occurs in
SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples from a wildlife-selling stall at the Huanan
market."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `well_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `moderate`
- **Critical framing:** co-presence, not infection. Brief 2's formulation should be reproduced
almost verbatim on the case page: *established* that susceptible mammals were present and
their genetic material occurs in positive samples; *not established* that one of them was
infected. Brief 1 preserves the memorable co-author characterization: the samples show
"the DNA and RNA ghosts of these animals."
- **Sources:** Crits-Christoph et al., Cell 2024; Liu et al., Nature 2023; SAGO 2025 (which
called the co-location the most compelling market evidence while declining to call it
proof).

**★ COV-C008 — No infected animal has been identified (negative evidence, zoonotic side)**
- **Wording:** "No animal sampled at the Huanan market, in its supply chain, or in China's
subsequent wildlife testing programs has been shown to carry SARS-CoV-2 or a sufficiently
close progenitor."
- **Rung:** observation (negative) · *Credibility:* `provisionally_supported`
- **Diagnosticity:** `moderate`
- **Why not `well_supported`:** the "roughly 80,000 animals tested, zero positives" figure is
China's own account to the 2021 WHO mission and has never been independently audited —
which cuts both ways: it is simultaneously the zoonotic case's principal empirical deficit
and a datum whose reliability the zoonotic side has reason to question. Brief 1 notes the
zero-positive result "sits oddly beside the documented reverse-zoonosis of the virus into
mink, deer and other species worldwide." SAGO itself called the absence "surprising,
considering the many susceptible species present."
- **Mitigating context to record alongside:** the market was cleared and disinfected before
sampling; the upstream farm investigation was never completed.
- **Sources:** WHO-China joint study 2021; SAGO 2025; Liu et al., Nature 2023; Science news
coverage of WHO's "inexcusable" statement.

**COV-C009 — An animal in the supply chain was infected before human market transmission**
- **Wording:** "At least one animal in the Huanan market supply chain was infected with
SARS-CoV-2 before sustained human transmission began at the market."
- **Rung:** attribution · *Credibility:* `unresolved` · *Diagnosticity:* `high` if resolved
- **Why keep an unresolved claim:** this is the zoonotic family's missing keystone, and naming
it explicitly is what stops the co-location evidence from being read as if it had already
established it. Brief 2 proposes exactly this node with "Status: unproven."
- **Sources:** Crits-Christoph et al., Cell 2024; Liu et al., Nature 2023; SAGO 2025.

### Theme: `lineages`

**★ COV-C010 — Two basal lineages, two nucleotides apart**
- **Wording:** "Pre-February-2020 SARS-CoV-2 genomes comprise two distinct lineages,
designated A and B, separated by two nucleotide differences."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `well_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `low`
- **Why separate from C011:** the observation is not in dispute; the inference from it is. This
is AGENTS.md §3.2 in practice — the rung split that stops evidence for the observation
counting as evidence for the inference.
- **Sources:** Pekar et al., Science 2022.

**★ COV-C011 — Earliest diversity better explained by two or more introductions**
- **Wording:** "The earliest SARS-CoV-2 genomic diversity is better explained by at least two
separate animal-to-human introductions than by a single introduction followed by early
diversification."
- **Rung:** mechanism · *Credibility:* `mixed` · *Diagnosticity:* `high`
- **Why high diagnosticity:** two independent introductions are easy under wildlife-trade
spillover and awkward under a single infected worker. SAGO reportedly cited the pattern as
distinguishing zoonosis from "what would be expected from a lab leak — which would be a
single virus introduction." If it falls, the zoonotic case retreats to "market
amplification plus unknown upstream event."
- **Why `mixed`:** it is model-based inference, sensitive to rooting choices, unsampled
intermediates, molecular-clock assumptions, and which early sequences are trusted; several
hundred early Chinese sequences remain unpublished; and there is an erratum in the vicinity
whose target must be pinned down (see §7). Brief 2's guidance is right: "Pekar et al.
proved two zoonoses" is too strong, and so is "all cases demonstrably descend from one
laboratory introduction."
- **Sources:** Pekar et al., Science 2022; Science erratum (DOI recorded in §5 — **which paper
it attaches to must be verified**); SAGO 2025; House Select Subcommittee majority report
2024 (asserting a single introduction — record as a disputed congressional interpretation,
in tension with the peer-reviewed analysis); Rootclaim response (asserts the erratum
"reduc[ed] its significance to negligible" — an advocacy characterization to check against
the erratum text); Rootclaim assertion that market cases are all lineage B while lineage A
cases occur outside the market, and the counter-assertion that the market lineage-A item is
a single environmental sample from 1 January.

### Theme: `genome`

**★ COV-C012 — The furin cleavage site is absent from known close relatives**
- **Wording:** "SARS-CoV-2 carries a 12-nucleotide insertion at the spike S1/S2 junction
encoding a polybasic furin cleavage motif that is absent from every known sarbecovirus,
including its closest sampled relatives."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `well_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `low`
- **Why low:** both briefs converge here, and it is the most important calibration point in
the case. The same observation is read as anomalous by one side and as within natural
variation by the other. Brief 1 records the specifics on both sides: for suspicion, the
site is absent from otherwise near-identical relatives and its two arginines use the
CGG-CGG doublet (~3% of the virus's arginine codons) common in mammalian expression
systems; against, furin motifs occur across other coronavirus lineages (HKU1, OC43,
MERS-CoV, merbecoviruses), the identical 12-nucleotide sequence reportedly occurs in
bat coronavirus Bat-HKU9, and the site is suboptimal — not what an engineer choosing from
known motifs would pick. Record the specific "not found in nature" formulation used by the
House majority report and the 2025 White House page as **contradicted as stated**.
- **Sources:** Andersen et al., Nature Medicine 2020; SAGO 2025; DEFUSE proposal 2018 (for the
conceptual overlap); Science Feedback review (natural-occurrence argument); Virological.org
post on the sarbecovirus origin of the FCS; a January 2026 peer-reviewed review reported to
conclude the evidence "does not permit definitive attribution"; FactCheck.org on the
five-point formulation.

**★ COV-C013 — No diagnostic engineering signature**
- **Wording:** "SARS-CoV-2's genome contains no feature presently accepted as diagnostic of
laboratory engineering."
- **Rung:** mechanism · *Credibility:* `well_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `moderate`
- **Essential caveat, to sit in `strongestObjection`:** "no diagnostic signature" and "no
research accident" are not the same statement. A naturally collected virus can leak without
modification, and seamless reverse genetics can leave no stereotyped scar — the ODNI itself
reported WIV possessed such methods while reporting no information they were applied to
SARS-CoV-2 or a close precursor. So this claim weighs against H5 specifically, and barely
at all against H3 or H4.
- **Sources:** Andersen et al., Nature Medicine 2020; ODNI June 2023 WIV report; ODNI October
2021 assessment; SAGO 2025.

**★ COV-C014 — Closest known relatives are too distant to be progenitors**
- **Wording:** "The closest known sampled relatives of SARS-CoV-2 — RaTG13 at roughly 96.2%
and BANAL-52 at roughly 96.8% whole-genome identity — are too genetically distant to be its
immediate progenitor."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `established` · *Diagnosticity:* `low`
- **Why it earns a slot anyway:** it is the shared premise that defeats several popular
arguments on both sides at once — the NIH-funded-virus-as-precursor claim, and the
RaTG13-as-the-leaked-virus claim. Brief 1 gives ~1,100 nucleotides of difference from
RaTG13, "implying decades of independent evolution."
- **Sources:** Zhou et al., Nature 2020; Temmam et al., Nature 2022 (BANAL); ODNI June 2023
report; NIH October 2021 statement.

**★ COV-C016 — Whether DEFUSE-like experiments were actually performed**

either way

**★ COV-C017 — DEFUSE backbones as a route to SARS-CoV-2**

- **Rung:** mechanism · *Credibility:* `provisionally_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `moderate`

**★ COV-C018 — WIV conducted SARS-like coronavirus work at BSL-2**
- **Wording:** "As of January 2019, Wuhan Institute of Virology researchers performed
experiments with SARS-like coronaviruses in BSL-2 laboratories."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `well_supported` (as an ODNI statement)
- **Diagnosticity:** `low`
- **Why low:** it establishes opportunity and degraded practice, which H3, H4, and H5 all share

- **Sources:** ODNI June 2023 WIV report (reported to state the BSL-2 finding "despite

**★ COV-C019 — No lab is known to have held SARS-CoV-2 or a close progenitor (negative evidence, research side)**
- **Wording:** "No laboratory is publicly known to have possessed SARS-CoV-2 or a sufficiently

- **Rung:** observation (negative) · *Credibility:* `well_supported` (as an IC statement)
- **Diagnosticity:** `moderate`

**★ COV-C020 — The WIV sample database went offline in September 2019**
- **Wording:** "The Wuhan Institute of Virology's principal bat-coronavirus sample database —
reported to hold records of roughly 22,000 samples and more than 100 unpublished sequences
— became unreachable from outside the institute on 12 September 2019 and has not since been
shared."
- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `provisionally_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `low`
*Split the fact from the motive.* The takedown as an event is the firmer half; the inference
from its timing is the softer one. The offered explanation — security measures following
hacking attempts — is unverifiable from outside, and unverifiability is not evidence of
concealment. Brief 1 calls the takedown "the case's most concrete unexplained act" while
also recording that the timing could coincide with a routine security review.
- **Sources:** Washington Post reporting; DRASTIC usage records as reported; ODNI June 2023
report; SAGO 2025 on unshared laboratory documentation.

**COV-C021 — Autumn-2019 WIV staff illnesses**
- **Wording:** "Several Wuhan Institute of Virology researchers were ill in autumn 2019 with

- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `well_supported` (as an IC characterization)
- **Diagnosticity:** `indeterminate`
- **Record explicitly as superseded:** the January 2021 State Department fact sheet's stronger

**COV-C022 — Mojiang mine and RaTG13 provenance**
- **Wording:** "The 2013 bat-coronavirus sample later designated RaTG13 was collected by the

- **Rung:** observation · *Credibility:* `provisionally_supported` · *Diagnosticity:* `low`
*Reads both ways, and the case page should say so plainly.* For the research-related family:
the WIV surveilled a site where a SARS-like virus plausibly sickened humans, held
unpublished sequences from it, and did not disclose the mine–RaTG13 connection in print

eight unpublished 2015 sequences are reported to sit in the offline database.
- **Sources:** Frontiers in Public Health analysis; Zhou et al., Nature 2020; Washington Post
reporting; the Kunming master's thesis (secondary description only — locate the original).

## 7. Traps and cautions

### 7.1 Where the briefs are tilted

- **Brief 1 leans lab-curious in emphasis, not in conclusion.** Its Sections 5–6 devote
  disproportionate space to conduct and hearings, and its section titles adopt
  investigative framings ("the circumstantial record," "the suppression claim"). It is
  scrupulous about recording counterweights, but the *shape* of the document gives the
  conduct thread roughly equal billing with the virology. On a case page that ratio would
  itself be an editorial claim. Its own §10 corrects for this by naming the conduct
  material as orthogonal — follow the §10 guidance, not the section balance.
- **Brief 1's citation practice is the bigger problem.** Its inline attributions are to
  outlets ("(The Washington Post)", "(Rev)", "(house.gov)", "(wikipedia.org)"), and its
  trailing link list mixes primary documents with aggregators, Wikipedia, an advocacy
  tracker (InfluenceWatch), a commercial transcript service (Rev), and a paywalled
  monitoring service. **Nothing may be cited from brief 1's chain without going to the
  underlying document.** Two of its citations are to Wikipedia articles.
- **Brief 2 leans institutional.** Its instinct is to defer to coordinated assessments and
  peer review, which makes it strong on primary sourcing and weak on the possibility that
  a coordinated assessment is wrong for structural reasons. Its own best insight cuts
  against it: SAGO leans zoonotic while depending almost entirely on data China has
  partly withheld. A page that treats SAGO as settling the question inherits that
  dependence silently.
- **The Rootclaim material is explicit advocacy** and says so: "we promise that those who
  make the effort … will be able to reach high confidence that Covid originated from a
  lab." Its base-rate argument about seafood-market clusters is a real challenge to the
  Huanan likelihood ratio and should be recorded as such (COV-C004) — but note that all
  its comparison outbreaks are genetic descendants of the Wuhan outbreak, not independent
  spillovers, which is a different reference class than the one the argument needs.
- **Neither brief examined the 18 MB debate PDF's contents**, and neither this digest nor
  any record should imply otherwise.

### 7.2 Claims that are popular but contradicted or overstated

Each of these should get a claim or an evidence record precisely *because* it is popular —
CONTENT_POLICY's fairness rule and AGENTS.md §3.11 both point the same way.

| Popular claim | Status per the briefs |
| --- | --- |
| "The furin cleavage site is not found in nature" | **Contradicted as stated.** Furin motifs occur across other coronavirus lineages. The defensible version is that it is absent from known *sarbecoviruses*, including SARS-CoV-2's closest sampled relatives. |
| "All cases descend from a single introduction" | **In tension with the peer-reviewed analysis.** Asserted in the House majority report; record it as a disputed congressional interpretation, not as a scientific finding. |
| "Sick WIV researchers in autumn 2019 prove an early lab infection" | **Deflated by the June 2023 ODNI report**, which concluded the episode "neither supports nor refutes either hypothesis." Brief 1 names it as one of the two weakest links in the lab case. |

### 7.3 Independence problems

This case has an unusually bad independence structure, and the evidence ledger must model
it explicitly (AGENTS.md §3.10):

- **The market evidence is one dataset, not three.** Worobey 2022, Liu 2023, and
  Crits-Christoph 2024 draw on overlapping Chinese CDC environmental sampling and case
  data. Crits-Christoph is a *reanalysis* of data underlying Liu, and shares authors with
  Worobey. Treat these as one independence group with three analytic layers, not three
  independent confirmations.
- **The two 2022 Science papers share authors and data.** Worobey and Pekar are companion
  papers with overlapping author lists and overlapping early-case inputs. Their agreement
  is not independent corroboration.
- **Everything rests on Chinese custodianship.** The case ascertainment, the environmental
  sampling, the animal testing counts, the early sequences, and the laboratory records all
  come from — or are withheld by — the same custodian. This is the deepest dependency in
  the case and it undercuts both hypotheses at once.
- **The ODNI June 2023 report is a single source doing enormous work.** Brief 2 cites it
  for sixteen distinct footnotes on both sides of the argument. A ledger that spawns a
  dozen evidence records from it must group them, and must cite its exculpatory statements
  with the same prominence as its incriminating ones — brief 2 says this explicitly.
- **Both camps cross-cite heavily, and the secondary layer is worse.** The lab-leak side
  cites DRASTIC, USRTK, and congressional releases which cite each other; the zoonosis side
  cites SAGO which cites the Science and Cell papers whose authors advised SAGO's inputs.
  Wire-copy duplication across outlets is one source with an independence group.
- **Preprint versus peer-reviewed versus press release must be visible on the record.** The
  July 2026 Slack material, the May 2026 testimony characterization, and the June 2026
  document release are all currently **press releases about documents**, not the documents.
  The 2026 Nature piece is a **comment**, not a research article or an institutional
  report. The Rootclaim material is a **blog post**. None of these is disqualifying; all of
  it must be labeled.
- **Corrections and errata get the same prominence as the findings.** The Worobey/Pekar
  erratum, the Lancet letter's competing-interests addendum, the deflation of the State
  Department fact sheet by the later ODNI report, and the former-SAGO-members' rebuttal of
  the DEFUSE route are all corrections — two weakening the conventional account, two
  weakening the contested one. Symmetry here is the test of whether the case page is honest.

### 7.4 Living-persons concerns

This case names many living people — scientists, officials, institution heads — and the
living-persons rules in AGENTS.md take precedence over completeness.

- **No claim in this case grades any person's culpability, honesty, or intent.** Not
  Fauci's, not Daszak's, not Shi Zhengli's, not the Proximal Origin authors'. The ladder
  grades evidence disputes: what a document states, what a dataset shows, whether a chain
  of inference holds. "Fauci prompted the paper to push a preferred narrative" is a
  congressional *assertion*, recorded as such with a locator, and set beside the minority
  report's contrary conclusion — it is not a claim to be graded.
- **Every institutional characterization stays attributed.** "The House majority concluded
  …", "ODNI leadership alleged …", "the committee chairman characterized …", "per WIV
  officials …". Brief 2's prefer/avoid table is a good drafting checklist and should be
  read before writing any of this case's prose.
- **Criminal proceedings.** Brief 2 reports an indictment and a guilty plea involving a
  named former official. If that thread is included at all, it appears only as: the
  admitted conduct, per the DOJ record, with the locator — and with the explicit statement
  that it establishes concealment of records, not anything about the biological origin, and
  nothing about any other person's knowledge. Any case page touching criminal charges
  displays the presumption of innocence and the corrections path.
- **Private communications.** Slack messages, private emails, and diary entries released
  by committees are quotable only from the released record, with the release as the
  locator, and only where the release itself is public. Do not reconstruct or paraphrase
  beyond what the released document shows. Do not treat a private probability estimate as
  a statement of belief about anything other than that moment.
- **The illness of named individuals.** The autumn-2019 WIV illness material concerns
  identifiable people's health. Record it at the level the ODNI states it — several
  researchers, symptoms characterized as nonspecific — and never speculate about
  individuals.
- **The scientists on both sides are doing science, not taking sides in a scandal.** Brief
  1 preserves an important observation: early-2020 entertaining of laboratory scenarios was
  normal scientific questioning that is now sometimes retro-presented as a signal of guilt.
  A case page that reproduces that move fails the constitution's rule against confusing
  personality with hypothesis.

### 7.5 Drafting cautions specific to this case

- **Freeze claim wording before the evidence pass** (AGENTS.md §3.12). This case is unusually
  prone to claim drift, because each observation has a strong and a defensible version that
  differ by one clause ("not found in nature" versus "absent from known sarbecoviruses").
  Freeze the defensible version, then gather.
- **Keep credibility and diagnosticity visibly separate.** This case is the clearest
  illustration in the whole atlas of why they are different axes: the furin cleavage site
  is a highly credible observation with near-zero diagnosticity, and the double-introduction
  inference is a moderately credible claim with high diagnosticity. If the UI ever collapses
  these, the case becomes misleading.
- **Do not convert secrecy into affirmative points.** Brief 2's evidence-density scale
  deliberately refuses to; the case should too. Non-production of records raises the
  `unresolved` count; it does not raise anyone's score.
- **Date every piece of evidence twice** — when the underlying event occurred and when the
  evidence became public. For DEFUSE, the 2020 communications, and the intelligence
  records, those differ by years, and the gap is itself part of several disputes.
