COVID-19 Origins: A Balanced Evidence Map and
Investigative Seed Report for Erebus
Executive summary
Scope and assumptions. This report is designed as a seed document for an Erebus case page as of
August 25, 2026. Because no target audience or length was specified, I assume a scientifically literate
general audience that may include researchers, journalists, policymakers, and readers approaching the
issue from competing prior beliefs. I use “research-related origin” as the broad category encompassing
accidental infection during field sampling, accidental exposure to a naturally occurring virus in a laboratory,
and accidental exposure to a manipulated or laboratory-adapted virus. Deliberate release is treated
separately. “Direct evidence” is used narrowly to mean evidence that actually connects a precursor virus,
infected animal, laboratory sample, or exposed worker to the first human infections—not merely evidence
of capability, opportunity, proximity, or misconduct.


That structure fits Erebus especially well. The site describes its purpose as decomposing controversial
hypotheses into atomic claims, mapping evidence for and against them symmetrically, preserving
provenance, and identifying potentially decisive evidence rather than operating as a “verdict machine.”     1




Bottom line. On the public scientific record, the strongest positive evidence currently points toward a
zoonotic emergence associated with the live-wildlife trade at or immediately upstream of Wuhan’s
Huanan Seafood Wholesale Market. The principal evidence is the geographic concentration of the earliest
known cases around the market; the concentration of SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples in the
market, including areas associated with live susceptible mammals; the documented sale of susceptible
wildlife there before closure; the co-occurrence in some SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples of
genetic material from animals such as raccoon dogs, civets, and bamboo rats; and phylogenetic work
interpreting the earliest viral diversity as more consistent with multiple animal-to-human introductions than
with a single sustained human introduction. 2


But the decisive zoonotic evidence has never been obtained: no animal sampled before or during the
market outbreak has been shown to carry SARS-CoV-2 or a sufficiently close progenitor whose sequence
establishes the transmission chain into humans. The Chinese CDC’s post-closure market investigation found
environmental contamination but did not establish an infected market animal, and the market had already
been closed and cleared when extensive sampling began. 3 WHO’s 2025 SAGO assessment therefore
concluded that the weight of accessible scientific evidence favors zoonotic spillover, while explicitly
declining to establish where, when, or through which animal the spillover occurred. SAGO described the co-
location of susceptible-animal genetic material and viral RNA as the most compelling market evidence, not
as proof of an infected animal. 4


At the same time, a research-related accident remains scientifically and investigatively unresolved,
principally because information capable of testing it has not been made available. The Wuhan Institute of
Virology conducted extensive pre-pandemic research on SARS-related bat coronaviruses, possessed

reverse-genetics and coronavirus-engineering capabilities, created chimeric SARS-like coronaviruses,
conducted some SARS-like coronavirus experiments under BSL-2 conditions, and had documented biosafety
concerns. Yet the U.S. Intelligence Community’s 2023 declassified report also said it had no indication that
WIV possessed SARS-CoV-2 or a close progenitor before the pandemic and no direct evidence of a
specific research-related incident that caused it. 5 The same report said illnesses among several WIV
employees in fall 2019 neither supported nor refuted either origin hypothesis because the reported
symptoms were nonspecific and some were inconsistent with COVID-19. 6


A particularly important piece of circumstantial laboratory-origin evidence is the 2018 DEFUSE proposal, an
EcoHealth-led application to DARPA involving WIV and other collaborators. The proposal contemplated
analyzing SARS-related coronavirus spike cleavage sites and, where mismatches existed, introducing
“appropriate human-specific cleavage sites” before testing growth in cell cultures. The proposal was
rejected; its existence establishes that this class of experiment was contemplated, not that it was actually
conducted or that it generated SARS-CoV-2. 7 In February 2026, 23 former WHO SAGO members argued
in Nature that even if DEFUSE had been funded, the specific proposed chimeric viral backbones and spike
components were too evolutionarily distant to be a plausible direct route to SARS-CoV-2. Their argument
significantly weakens a simplistic “DEFUSE was the blueprint for SARS-CoV-2” claim, although it does not
resolve the broader question of whether other, undocumented research involving a different virus could
have occurred. 8


The genome does not provide a presently accepted forensic signature of engineering. SARS-CoV-2’s
furin cleavage site and other features attracted legitimate early scrutiny, and seamless reverse genetics can
in principle leave no obvious engineering scar. Nevertheless, the 2023 coordinated ODNI report stated that
almost all U.S. intelligence agencies assessed SARS-CoV-2 was not genetically engineered, most assessed it
was not laboratory-adapted, and all assessed it was not developed as a biological weapon. 9 Natural
relatives such as RaTG13 and BANAL-52 demonstrate substantial unsampled natural sarbecovirus diversity
and show that SARS-CoV-2-like receptor-binding properties can evolve in wildlife, but neither is close
enough to be the immediate progenitor. 10


The strongest recent revelations concerning Anthony Fauci, NIAID, EcoHealth, and congressional
oversight concern transparency, conflicts, records handling, grant oversight, and the integrity of the
investigative process—not the biological origin itself. HHS OIG found serious failures by NIH and
EcoHealth in monitoring the relevant grants and subawards. 11 Congressional records document that
Fauci participated in early discussions in which laboratory scenarios were seriously considered, while the
authors of the eventual Proximal Origin paper subsequently concluded that purposeful manipulation was
implausible. Republican and Democratic congressional investigators drew sharply different conclusions
about whether that evolution reflected improper pressure or ordinary scientific reassessment. 12


A major development after the 2024 hearings is no longer merely an allegation: on August 18, 2026, former
NIAID senior adviser David Morens pleaded guilty to a conspiracy involving evasion of FOIA and federal-
records requirements in communications about coronavirus grants. DOJ states that Morens and co-
conspirators intentionally moved relevant communications to personal email, exchanged nonpublic NIH
information, attempted to influence restoration of a coronavirus grant, and used back channels; the plea
also describes an illegal-gratuity arrangement associated with a scientific commentary favoring natural
origins. This is powerful evidence of records and ethics misconduct in the NIH/EcoHealth orbit. It does not
demonstrate that SARS-CoV-2 came from a laboratory, and it does not by itself establish that Fauci
directed or knew of the criminal conduct. 13

Likewise, Fauci’s July 29, 2026 Senate appearance, at which he invoked the Fifth Amendment rather than

affirmative evidence of a laboratory origin. The committee is disputing the privilege in light of Fauci’s
January 2025 pardon and has pursued contempt proceedings; that legal dispute should be kept separate
from the scientific question of viral origin. 14


The U.S. intelligence picture has also been internally divided. In 2021, four Intelligence Community
elements plus the National Intelligence Council favored natural exposure with low confidence, while one
element favored a laboratory-associated incident with moderate confidence. 15 By the 2023 coordinated
report, the Department of Energy and FBI favored a laboratory-associated incident, while the NIC and four
other agencies favored natural exposure and CIA plus another agency remained unable to decide. 16 In
June 2026, ODNI leadership released documents under the categorical heading “Fauci Funded Wuhan Lab
Research That Sparked COVID” and alleged improper influence over intelligence assessments. Because that
public release is not itself presented as a new coordinated Intelligence Community estimate, a neutral
site should distinguish the current executive-branch allegation from the evidentiary status of the underlying
documents and from earlier coordinated assessments. 17


The current WHO position is consequently more nuanced than either “zoonosis proven” or “lab leak proven.”
SAGO’s 2025 scientific assessment found the weight of accessible scientific evidence favors zoonosis,
but WHO Director-General Tedros said crucial information remains unavailable—including hundreds of
early viral sequences, more detailed market-animal information, and Wuhan laboratory work and biosafety
information—and therefore all hypotheses must remain on the table. 18


A defensible Erebus top-line formulation is therefore:


                         The best-developed affirmative scientific case currently favors zoonotic emergence
                         connected to the Huanan wildlife trade, but no infected source animal or immediate
                         progenitor has been identified. Research-related scenarios remain plausible but lack
                         publicly disclosed direct evidence linking a pre-pandemic Wuhan laboratory virus or
                         infected worker to the outbreak. Recent oversight proceedings have materially
                         strengthened the case that grant oversight and federal-records handling were
                         deficient, but those findings should not be converted into biological-origin evidence
                         without an additional causal link. 19

A useful site-design choice is to attach two dates to important pieces of evidence: the date the underlying
event occurred and the date the evidence became publicly available. DEFUSE, Fauci-era emails, and
intelligence records illustrate why those can differ by years.


Zoonotic-origin evidence
The zoonotic case is strongest when stated narrowly. It does not require claiming that scientists have found
“the animal that gave humans COVID.” Rather, it argues that several independent observations are
substantially more expected under a wildlife-trade-associated spillover than under many alternative
scenarios.


The early geographic signal. Worobey and colleagues reconstructed the locations of early known
December 2019 COVID-19 cases and found a strong concentration around the Huanan Seafood Wholesale
Market. Crucially, their argument was not limited to cases already known to have market exposure; cases
without a recognized market link also clustered geographically around it. The authors further mapped early
SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples inside the market and found concentrations in the part of the
market associated with wildlife sales. 43 A 2024 erratum should be linked alongside the original paper on
any rigorous case page rather than allowing readers to assume the initial publication is the entire record.
 44




This pattern has at least three possible interpretations: the market was the location of the first animal-to-
human spillover; spillover happened immediately upstream in the wildlife supply chain and infected animals
arrived at Huanan; or the market was an unusually effective amplification site for a virus already
circulating among humans. The first two are zoonotic-origin variants; the third allows an origin elsewhere.
The spatial data distinguish these possibilities only imperfectly.




                                                     7
The environmental-market signal. Chinese CDC investigators collected 923 environmental samples
beginning after market closure and detected SARS-CoV-2 in the market environment. They were unable to
demonstrate an infected source animal from the animals sampled after closure. 3 This is important
because environmental RNA alone cannot identify who deposited it: an infected vendor, customer, market
animal, contaminated surface, or some combination.


The 2024 Cell analysis materially sharpened this evidence. Crits-Christoph and colleagues reanalyzed the
market metagenomic sequencing data and found that some samples positive for SARS-CoV-2 RNA also
contained mitochondrial DNA from susceptible wildlife, including raccoon dogs, civets, and bamboo rats,
with particularly relevant samples associated with wildlife-selling locations. 37 Co-location makes an
animal source more plausible than viral RNA alone does. It still cannot tell whether viral RNA came from
the animal whose DNA was present or from an infected human standing in the same stall.


That distinction should be made explicit on Erebus:


       Established: susceptible mammals were present in the relevant market environment, and
       their genetic material occurs in some SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples.
       Not established: one of those mammals was infected with SARS-CoV-2 before human market
       transmission began. 45


WHO SAGO’s 2025 assessment made essentially this distinction, describing the co-location as the most
compelling evidence of possible spillover while declining to call it direct proof. 46


Early viral diversity. Pekar and colleagues reconstructed the earliest viral phylogeny and argued that the
two basal early lineages commonly designated A and B are most consistent with at least two zoonotic
introductions, each associated with the Huanan market. 47 If correct, two independent introductions
from market animals are substantially harder to reconcile with a single infected laboratory worker. But the
evidentiary weight depends on phylogenetic reconstruction, assumptions about unsampled intermediate
viruses, molecular timing, and which early sequences are treated as reliable. It should therefore be
classified as model-based evidence, not as direct observation of two spillovers.


A neutral page should resist the symmetrical overstatements:


       “Pekar et al. proved two zoonoses” is too strong.


       “All cases demonstrably descend from one laboratory introduction” is also too strong.


The House majority’s 2024 conclusion that the data establish one human introduction is in tension with the
peer-reviewed Pekar analysis and should be presented as a disputed congressional interpretation, not as an
uncontested scientific fact. 48


Natural evolutionary context. The initial WIV paper identified RaTG13 as a bat SARS-related coronavirus
with approximately 96.2% whole-genome similarity to SARS-CoV-2. Later sampling in Laos identified
BANAL viruses, including BANAL-52 at roughly 96.8% similarity, and experimentally demonstrated that
some related bat-virus receptor-binding domains can engage human ACE2 efficiently. 49 These




                                                        8
discoveries are important because they show that key SARS-CoV-2-like molecular properties exist in
naturally circulating sarbecoviruses and that the natural viral diversity is incompletely sampled.


They do not solve the origin. A virus at 96–97% whole-genome identity is far too divergent to be the
immediate ancestor of SARS-CoV-2. The 2023 ODNI report explicitly notes that neither RaTG13 nor
BANAL-52 is close enough to be a direct progenitor. 50 Thus:


       Evidence for a natural evolutionary reservoir is strong. Evidence identifying the actual
       immediate natural precursor is absent.


The furin cleavage site and other genomic features. SARS-CoV-2 possesses a functionally important
cleavage site in spike that was not present in its closest known sampled sarbecovirus relatives, making it a
legitimate feature to investigate. Early Proximal Origin analysis argued that SARS-CoV-2’s genome did not
look like a virus deliberately designed from known backbones, and the broader subsequent natural-virus
record has expanded the range of observed coronavirus biology. 51 WHO SAGO concluded in 2025 that
the virus’s notable genomic features can be explained through ordinary mutation and recombination and
do not themselves establish a man-made origin, while also recognizing that genomic data alone cannot
logically exclude every laboratory scenario. 52


That last clause matters. “No diagnostic engineering signature” and “therefore no research accident” are not
equivalent statements. A naturally collected virus could leak without engineering, and some modern
reverse-genetics methods can produce constructs without a stereotyped scar. ODNI itself reported that WIV
scientists possessed reverse-genetics methods capable of seamless manipulation, while simultaneously
stating that it had no information that those techniques were applied to SARS-CoV-2 or a sufficiently close
precursor. 5


What most weakens the zoonotic case is not a contrary positive finding but missing direct evidence.
No pre-outbreak farm, trader, market animal, or wildlife sample has yielded SARS-CoV-2’s immediate
progenitor. WHO says it has not received all the detailed animal-market data and early sequences it
requested. 40 The proper evidentiary interpretation is therefore “strong circumstantial convergence
with an unresolved transmission link,” not “proven.”

sampling, not only incidents physically inside a laboratory. 53 For Erebus, the hypothesis should be split
at least as follows.

Why a research accident is biologically plausible. WIV did relevant work. The 2023 ODNI report says its
scientists conducted extensive coronavirus sampling and genetic analysis, created chimeric SARS-like
coronaviruses, used reverse-genetics methods, and maintained large bat-sample collections. It also says
some researchers conducted SARS-like coronavirus experiments in BSL-2 laboratories and that biosafety
practices were inadequate at least some of the time, increasing the risk of accidental exposure. 5 Those
facts establish capability and opportunity. They do not establish causation.


The same ODNI document provides some of the most important counterevidence to a strong laboratory-
origin claim. It states that the Intelligence Community had no indication that WIV’s pre-pandemic
holdings included SARS-CoV-2 or a close progenitor and no direct evidence of a specific research-
related incident capable of causing the pandemic. It also says known WIV coronavirus work with military-
associated researchers involved viruses too distant to be the SARS-CoV-2 precursor. 54 A site that cites
ODNI for WIV biosafety concerns should cite these statements with equal prominence.


The NIH–EcoHealth–WIV research chain is real but often described imprecisely. NIH project
R01AI110964, led by EcoHealth Alliance, investigated bat-coronavirus emergence, including field sampling,
receptor binding, viral characterization, humanized-mouse work, and laboratory experiments intended to
estimate spillover risk; WIV was among the subrecipients. 55 This establishes a U.S.-funded research




                                                       10
relationship relevant to the subject matter of the pandemic. It does not establish that U.S. funding paid for
creation of SARS-CoV-2.


The “gain-of-function” dispute needs unusually careful wording. In a broad scientific sense, a manipulated
organism that acquires greater replication, host range, or another measurable function can reasonably be
described as having gained a function. U.S. regulatory frameworks for enhanced potential pandemic
pathogens are narrower. Congressional investigators emphasized Lawrence Tabak’s acknowledgement that
some WIV experiments could be called gain-of-function in the broad sense, while NIH had earlier concluded
the proposal did not meet the regulatory threshold requiring P3CO review. 56 HHS OIG found that NIH
and EcoHealth failed in grant monitoring and that a late progress report disclosed an experiment reaching
a grant-specific growth threshold that NIH had required EcoHealth to report. 33


For an evidence page, the safe formulation is:


       Some NIH-supported WIV experiments can be described as gain-of-function in the
       generic sense of producing increased viral function. Whether they met the narrower
       federal ePPP/P3CO regulatory category was a separate regulatory judgment. Neither
       semantic question establishes that the experiments produced SARS-CoV-2.


DEFUSE is genuine circumstantial evidence, but its inferential reach should be bounded. The March
2018 EcoHealth-led proposal to DARPA proposed extensive bat coronavirus work. One section states that
researchers would examine proteolytic and potential furin cleavage sites and, where clear mismatches
existed, “introduce appropriate human-specific cleavage sites” and assess viral growth. 7 That is notable
because SARS-CoV-2 later emerged with an unusual cleavage site in spike.


What DEFUSE establishes:


    1. Relevant investigators had contemplated experimental alteration of cleavage sites in SARS-related
       coronaviruses before the pandemic. 7
    2. The proposed consortium connected EcoHealth, WIV, and U.S. coronavirus expertise. 57
    3. The conceptual overlap makes it reasonable to investigate whether related experiments occurred
       under other funding or before/after the rejected proposal.

What it does not establish is at least as important: DARPA did not fund the proposal; there is no public
record proving the proposed cleavage-site experiment was carried out; and there is no published precursor
sequence connecting those proposed constructs to SARS-CoV-2. 58


The 2026 Nature article by 23 former SAGO members adds a substantial technical counterargument. They
state that the specific coronavirus backbones and spike components contemplated in DEFUSE belong to
viral lineages too distant from SARS-CoV-2 for the proposed constructs to plausibly be its direct precursor
and clarify that some claims about a self-spreading coronavirus vaccine misread the proposal. 8 A
balanced page should therefore classify DEFUSE as medium-value circumstantial evidence of research
intent/capability, but low-value direct evidence of SARS-CoV-2 ancestry.


Reported WIV employee illnesses are weak evidence in their current form. ODNI confirmed several WIV
researchers were ill in fall 2019 but said their symptoms were compatible with many conditions, some were
not characteristic of COVID-19, there was no indication relevant researchers were hospitalized for a COVID-




                                                      11
like illness, and some illnesses were attributable to other conditions. The IC explicitly concluded that the
illness information neither supports nor refutes either origin hypothesis. 6 This item becomes highly
important only if primary medical records, stored sera, PCR material, or a documented seroconversion show
SARS-CoV-2 or a close progenitor infection before the recognized outbreak.


Intelligence assessments add information but not publicly auditable proof. The 2023 coordinated
ODNI report says differences among agencies arise from how they weigh intelligence reporting, scientific
publications, conflicting reporting, and major information gaps. NIC and four agencies favored natural
exposure; DOE and FBI favored a laboratory-associated incident; CIA and another agency were unable to
choose. 16 The correct inference is not that “intelligence proves lab leak,” nor that “most agencies prove
zoonosis.” It is that analysts with access to substantial nonpublic information remained divided and that
neither side had diagnostic evidence.


The June 2026 ODNI document release deserves a prominent but separate evidence node. ODNI leadership
publicly alleged that Fauci-funded research “sparked COVID” and that Fauci improperly influenced
intelligence assessments; the released index includes items such as a 2020 Lawrence Livermore analysis,
Fauci briefing records, interagency emails, and congressional correspondence. 17 These records warrant
primary-document analysis. Until a new coordinated estimate, transparent analytical memorandum, or
direct origin evidence is produced, however, the headline characterization should be labeled as the
position of the releasing administration/ODNI leadership, not silently converted into a consensus IC
finding.


The laboratory-origin case is consequently strongest in this form:


       Relevant coronavirus research occurred in Wuhan; some practices created genuine exposure
       risks; sophisticated engineering capabilities existed; a rejected pre-pandemic proposal
       contemplated experiments with an intriguing overlap to a SARS-CoV-2 feature; grant
       oversight was deficient; and crucial laboratory records remain unavailable. 59


Its central weakness is equally simple:


       No publicly disclosed pre-pandemic WIV virus, construct, raw sequence, frozen sample,
       infected worker, or documented laboratory incident has been shown to be the ancestor
       or source of SARS-CoV-2. 60

Origin evidence asks what infected the first humans.
Research-capability evidence asks what laboratories were able or planning to do.
Process-integrity evidence asks whether officials preserved records, disclosed conflicts, and answered
investigators accurately.
Assessment evidence consists of conclusions drawn by scientists, intelligence agencies, congressional
committees, or executive officials.

The drafting of Proximal Origin deserves scrutiny without mind-reading. The published paper appeared
on March 17, 2020 and strongly argued that the available genomic features did not support purposeful
manipulation. 27 Later released communications and congressional interviews establish that some
authors initially regarded laboratory explanations as sufficiently plausible to investigate and that Fauci,
Collins, Farrar, and others were engaged in discussions around the issue. Andersen testified extensively
about the evolution of his thinking and maintained that the paper was an effort to test hypotheses against
the available evidence. 35


Two incompatible claims are often made from that record:


       “The scientists considered lab origin privately, therefore their later natural-origin
       conclusion was fraudulent.”


That does not follow: changing a hypothesis after analysis is normal science.


       “Because the paper was peer reviewed, the communications around its formation are
       irrelevant.”


That also goes too far: provenance, conflicts, outside input, and the relationship between private
uncertainty and public confidence are legitimate subjects of scrutiny.


The Erebus node should therefore ask narrower questions: Who proposed the paper? Who edited or
commented on it? What evidence caused each author’s posterior view to change? Was any governmental
official given approval power? Were grants promised or discussed in connection with conclusions? Is there
documentary evidence of coercion or quid pro quo? The House majority says the record supports improper
influence; the minority says it does not. 38 The documents should sit above both interpretations.


The Morens plea significantly changes the status of one branch of the controversy. In 2024, House
investigators alleged that Morens used private email, deleted or concealed federal records, and acted as a
back channel to EcoHealth. 65 By August 2026, DOJ reported a guilty plea admitting a conspiracy to evade
FOIA and federal-records requirements after NIH terminated the bat-coronavirus grant. The admitted
conduct included hidden personal-email communications, nonpublic NIH information, efforts to influence
funding, and back-channel communications to a senior NIAID official. 13


For Erebus this should trigger an evidence-status update:

But the next inference still requires evidence:

That causal bridge remains missing.


The July 2026 Fauci hearing likewise belongs primarily in a transparency branch. The Senate resolution
states that Fauci delivered prepared testimony and then asserted the Fifth Amendment to questions
relevant to COVID origins, risky life-sciences research, and his federal tenure; the committee chairman
rejected the privilege as applied to pardoned conduct and moved toward contempt. 66 Whatever the
eventual legal resolution, a neutral evidence system should not assign the invocation a positive laboratory-
origin score. It instead changes the status of an investigative question from “testimony available” to
“testimony unavailable/disputed.”


The 2026 CIA/ODNI developments could become more consequential if their underlying records establish
one of the missing causal links. Chair Paul’s May hearing statement says CIA scientific analysts repeatedly
found a laboratory leak most likely but that those judgments were buried or softened. 67 The June ODNI
release similarly alleges Fauci influence on intelligence analysis. 68 The appropriate next move is not to
decide the origin from those characterizations; it is to obtain the actual analytic drafts, sourcing, dissent
memoranda, meeting records, reviewer comments, and final decision chain.

The reason Huanan-associated zoonosis receives the highest evidence-density score is the convergence of
geospatial epidemiology, environmental sampling, susceptible-animal presence, wildlife genetic material
and phylogenetic analyses. 75 The two laboratory-accident categories receive non-zero scores because
relevant research, engineering capabilities, biosafety concerns, DEFUSE and incomplete records are real;
they do not receive higher scores because no pre-pandemic close precursor or causative exposure has been
publicly demonstrated. 83 Deliberate release receives zero because no affirmative public evidence has
been established and coordinated intelligence assessments reject the biological-weapon claim. 16


This scoring system should ideally be supplemented on the live site with separate indicators for direct
evidence, circumstantial evidence, source quality, replicability, missing-data sensitivity, and
discriminating power. That would prevent a stack of many weak facts from looking equivalent to one

The investigation should apply the same evidentiary standard symmetrically. For zoonosis, do not accept
“markets commonly cause spillovers” as proof that this market caused this spillover. For laboratory origin,
do not accept “WIV performed risky coronavirus research” as proof that this research produced this virus.
The decisive question in both cases is causal linkage.


For the Erebus case page, the most useful top-level question is not “Did COVID come from a lab?” A more
precise title would be:


       What event initiated sustained human transmission of SARS-CoV-2 in late 2019?


That avoids conflating COVID-19, the disease, with SARS-CoV-2, the virus, and permits genuinely distinct
hypotheses to be compared.

The page should also display provenance and epistemic status separately. A useful evidence-card
schema would be:


claim → evidence item → source type → primary/secondary → date created → date
disclosed → supports/opposes → direct/circumstantial/process-integrity → source
reliability → alternative explanations → missing discriminator → current status


That matches Erebus’s stated design goal of showing where disagreement lives rather than compressing
contested evidence into a verdict. 1


Finally, the case should maintain three visually distinct conclusions rather than one global “lab vs. animal”
meter:


Biological-origin layer: published positive evidence currently favors Huanan-associated zoonosis, without
direct animal proof. 89


Research-accident layer: a laboratory or field-research accident remains plausible because relevant
capabilities, activities and biosafety concerns existed, but no publicly disclosed pre-outbreak precursor or
causative infection has linked WIV to SARS-CoV-2. 5


Institutional-integrity layer: oversight failures and some records misconduct are no longer speculative;
HHS OIG documented grant-monitoring failures, and Morens has admitted a federal records/FOIA
conspiracy. Broader allegations that Fauci or intelligence officials deliberately suppressed the true origin
remain contested and require document-specific proof. 90

That separation is the most important safeguard against both forms of motivated reasoning in the debate:
institutional misconduct does not prove a laboratory origin, and strong market evidence does not
retroactively erase legitimate questions about risky research, missing records, conflicts, or
government transparency. WHO’s current position captures the resulting epistemic state well: the
accessible scientific evidence points more strongly toward zoonotic spillover, but critical animal, early-
sequence, laboratory and intelligence information is still unavailable, so the origin remains unresolved
rather than conclusively adjudicated. 91
