THE MAGIC T-SHIRT 
An outward pressure wave 
Watch the shirt. Watch the movement of the fabric itself. 
The shirt rapidly deforms, moving outwards and upwards. At one point the neckline of the shirt is approximately level with Kirk's left ear. Finally, the bleeding from the neck appears after the shockwave, heavily suggesting that whatever caused the shockwave was a precursor to whatever caused the neck injury, rather than the other way round. 
What we're seeing here suggests an outward explosive force.

How shaped charges work 
The video above shows a traditional shaped charge, the kind used to penetrate tank armor. These use a cone of metal (usually copper) backed by high explosive. When the explosive detonates, it collapses the cone inward at extreme velocity, forming a jet of superplastic metal that can punch through steel plate like it's cardboard. 
The principle was discovered by accident in the 1880s and has been refined for over a century. The physics are well understood, the engineering is mature, and the effects are devastating. 
Explosives work at many different scales. If you need to punch through a foot of steel, you need a lot of explosives. If you need to punch through human flesh, and the explosive is directly against the flesh, you need only a few grams of explosives.

Video analysis shows that Kirk's microphone was oriented edge-on to his body — not flat against his chest. This is consistent with a shaped charge design, where the cone is oriented along the length of the device and the jet fires inward from the edge toward the body.
