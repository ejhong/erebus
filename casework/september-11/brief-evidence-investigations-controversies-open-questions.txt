September 11, 2001: Evidence, Investigations,
Controversies, and Open Questions
Executive summary
The September 11, 2001 attacks are unusually well documented for a historical event of their scale. The
public record includes the bipartisan National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States,
congressional investigations, the FBI's PENTTBOM criminal investigation, Federal Aviation Administration
and military records, thousands of exhibits from United States v. Zacarias Moussaoui, structural and fire
investigations by the National Institute of Standards and Technology, Department of Justice and Central
Intelligence Agency inspector-general reviews, declassified intelligence records, civil litigation, military-
commission proceedings, and a large technical and academic literature. The 9/11 Commission alone
reviewed more than 2.5 million pages and interviewed more than 1,200 people in ten countries; NIST's
engineering investigation involved more than 200 professionals, interviews with more than 1,000 building
occupants and more than 100 responders, extensive photographic and video evidence, recovered structural
steel, laboratory testing, and computer modeling. 1


The best-supported historical account is that al-Qaeda organized the attacks; Khalid Sheikh Mohammed
developed the core aircraft-as-weapons concept; Osama bin Laden approved and supported the operation;
Mohamed Atta became its tactical leader in the United States; and nineteen hijackers commandeered
American Airlines Flights 11 and 77 and United Airlines Flights 175 and 93. The FBI officially identified all
nineteen, while the 9/11 Commission reconstructed the plot through immigration, financial, travel,
communications, intelligence, eyewitness, flight-control, and captured-al-Qaeda evidence. 2


At 8:46:40 a.m. Eastern time, Flight 11 struck the World Trade Center's North Tower; Flight 175 struck the
South Tower at 9:03:11; Flight 77 struck the Pentagon at 9:37:46; and Flight 93 crashed near Shanksville,
Pennsylvania, at 10:03:11 after passengers and crew attempted to retake the aircraft. The South Tower
collapsed at approximately 9:59 and the North Tower at approximately 10:28. World Trade Center Building
7, which was not struck by an aircraft, collapsed at about 5:20 p.m. after uncontrolled fires had burned for
hours. 3


The attacks killed 2,977 victims, excluding the nineteen hijackers: 2,753 in New York, 184 at the Pentagon/
aboard Flight 77, and 40 aboard Flight 93. The National September 11 Memorial & Museum records victims
from ninety countries and 441 first responders killed on the day. 4


On the engineering question, NIST concluded that the Twin Towers' collapses began after aircraft impacts
severed and damaged structural members, dislodged fireproofing, and ignited multi-floor fires that
progressively weakened the remaining structure. NIST investigated the factors leading to collapse
initiation rather than attempting to numerically simulate every stage of the towers' subsequent
destruction. Independent engineering papers by Bažant and colleagues and by Usmani, Chung, and Torero
analyzed progressive-collapse and fire-induced instability mechanisms, differing in some mechanical details
while remaining compatible with impact/fire-initiated collapse rather than explosive demolition. 5




                                                      1
For WTC 7, NIST concluded that uncontrolled fires produced thermal expansion and cascading floor failures
around Column 79, leading to progressive structural failure and global collapse. NIST explicitly investigated
explosive-demolition scenarios and reported neither recorded nor witness evidence of the blast levels its
calculations indicated would have been necessary. Its modeling nevertheless contains uncertainties, and
NIST itself acknowledges differences between portions of its global-collapse simulation and the exterior
deformation visible on video. 6 A University of Alaska Fairbanks project led by J. Leroy Hulsey reached the
contrary conclusion that fire did not cause the observed collapse and proposed near-simultaneous column
failures as necessary to reproduce it. That study deserves inclusion as a technically substantive dissenting
source, but its evidentiary weight should be presented cautiously: the project was funded with $316,153
from Architects & Engineers for 9/11 Truth, an advocacy organization committed to challenging the
official explanation, and its final report is not equivalent to a consensus professional-society or NIST
investigation. The project does, however, make an unusually large quantity of modeling data publicly
available. 7


A related thermite hypothesis derives partly from a 2009 paper by Niels Harrit and coauthors reporting red-
gray chips with energetic properties in privately collected WTC dust samples. The paper is worth linking
because excluding primary dissenting literature would make an evidence-oriented case page weaker, not
stronger. But it should be labeled separately from official forensic evidence: the samples were not collected
through a controlled governmental forensic chain of custody, and the claimed interpretation has not
displaced the extensive impact/fire-collapse literature. NIST also should not be misrepresented: it did not
test tower steel for thermite or explosive residues; instead, it rejected demolition explanations on
structural, physical, visual, acoustic, and feasibility grounds. 8


The strongest genuinely unresolved issues are therefore not whether aircraft struck the targets or whether
al-Qaeda organized the operation. They concern the boundaries of the conspiracy and the quality of pre-
attack intelligence: precisely what outside assistance the hijackers received; whether people associated
with Saudi governmental institutions knowingly supported Nawaf al-Hazmi and Khalid al-Mihdhar; why
intelligence about the pair was not disseminated and acted upon sooner; the counterfactual significance of
the Phoenix memo and Zacarias Moussaoui investigation; the ultimate origin of the funds that al-Qaeda
used for the operation; and how much confidence should be assigned to details reconstructed from post-
capture interrogations conducted under coercive conditions. The Justice Department inspector general
found serious systemic FBI failures involving the Phoenix memo, Moussaoui, and Hazmi/Mihdhar while
expressly declining to conclude that correct handling would necessarily have prevented the attacks. 9


The Saudi-support question has changed significantly since the original investigations. The 2004
Commission reported that it had not found evidence that the Saudi government as an institution or senior
Saudi officials individually funded al-Qaeda, while former Senator Bob Graham and others argued that
possible foreign support deserved deeper investigation. Subsequent FBI releases under declassification
initiatives and years of civil discovery have produced additional material. On August 28, 2025, a federal
judge rejected Saudi Arabia's renewed attempt to dismiss claims by 9/11 victims and families, permitting
the litigation to move forward; crucially, the ruling concerned jurisdiction and the legal sufficiency of the
evidence to proceed, not a final adjudication that Saudi Arabia was responsible for the attacks. Saudi
Arabia denies the allegations. 10


The criminal and quasi-criminal record likewise remains incomplete. Zacarias Moussaoui pleaded guilty to
terrorism offenses and was sentenced on May 4, 2006 to life imprisonment without possibility of release;
the federal court subsequently placed more than 1,200 admitted trial exhibits online, making that archive

rulings, suppression litigation, and appellate filings in the KSM case. 12

Scope, method, and source hierarchy
The page should answer five different questions rather than implicitly treating them as one: what
happened; who carried it out; how the physical destruction occurred; what authorities knew
beforehand and how they responded; and which material questions remain unresolved. Each question
has a different optimal source base. The 9/11 Commission is strongest on plot reconstruction and federal
response; NIST on building performance; FBI and federal-court records on criminal evidence; congressional

The single most important editorial rule should be claim-source fit. The Commission's finding that
nineteen al-Qaeda hijackers conducted the attacks does not itself prove NIST's finite-element assumptions
about WTC 7; NIST's structural analysis says nothing about Saudi facilitation; an FBI intelligence memo does
not establish the thermodynamic properties of WTC dust; and a civil court's refusal to dismiss a case is not
equivalent to a verdict on the merits. Keeping those domains separate will make the case page substantially
stronger. 26

Contemporaneous journalism deserves a distinct track. The Washington Post's September 11–12 coverage
reported four hijacked aircraft, destruction of the Twin Towers, the Pentagon strike, the Pennsylvania crash,
initial suspicion of bin Laden's network, and the chaotic emergency response. It also illustrates why
contemporaneous sources need date labels: early reports contained casualty estimates and other details
that were later corrected. 37


For the site, display contemporaneous articles under a heading such as “What was known at the time”,
next to a later “What subsequent investigation established” panel. That makes evolving knowledge
visible rather than quietly substituting hindsight for contemporary understanding.

The timeline should exist as structured data rather than only prose. Recommended fields are id ,
timestamp , timezone , event_type , location , entities , summary , confidence ,
primary_source , and notes . On attack-day entries, preserve seconds where the official record
supports them; on reconstructed pre-attack events, use date ranges rather than artificial precision.

Engineering findings, major hypotheses, and critical perspectives
The central engineering question is not whether ordinary office fires can melt structural steel. NIST does
not claim the tower steel melted. Its mechanism depends on mechanical damage plus loss of
fireproofing plus heating-induced weakening, deformation, load redistribution and instability. NIST's
tower work included tests of steel at elevated temperature and integrated metallurgical findings with fire
and structural models. 80


For the Twin Towers, the sequence supported by NIST is approximately:


Aircraft impact → severed/damaged columns and floors → widespread dislodging of sprayed fire-
resistant material → multi-floor fires → heating and weakening/sagging → inward bowing and load
redistribution → column instability → collapse initiation → gravity-driven progressive destruction.
NIST concluded that the interval before initiation depended substantially on how the fires evolved through
the damaged floors. 88


That explanation should be accompanied by independent engineering literature rather than presented
solely as an appeal to NIST. Bažant and Zhou's early Journal of Engineering Mechanics analysis argued that,
once a sufficiently large upper section began descending, the remaining structure could not arrest
progressive collapse. Later Bažant work developed the mechanics further. 89 Usmani, Chung and Torero
independently used finite-element analysis to explore fire-induced instability mechanisms; their model
deliberately omitted aircraft-impact structural damage, making it valuable as a demonstration that heating
itself can generate destabilizing structural behavior rather than as a full reconstruction of the actual towers.
 90




For WTC 7, NIST's mechanism is different because no aircraft struck the building. Debris from WTC 1
damaged WTC 7 and ignited fires; because water supplies were compromised, fires burned uncontrolled.
NIST concluded that thermal expansion of long-span floor members precipitated a connection failure near
Column 79; cascading floor failures deprived the column of lateral support, Column 79 buckled, interior
failures propagated, the east penthouse descended, and global exterior collapse followed. 55


NIST's treatment of WTC 7 includes an important point often lost in polemics: its own FAQ acknowledges
that late-stage simulated exterior deformation does not visually reproduce every feature of the video. NIST
argues that the model does reproduce the important initiation sequence and timing and says uncertainty
rises greatly once global collapse and falling debris interactions begin. 34 This is a legitimate model
limitation to expose on the page; it is not, by itself, evidence that the initial failure mechanism was false.

The insider-trading controversy is a good example of how the site's ontology should distinguish
propositions. “Some pre-9/11 options trading was statistically unusual” has peer-reviewed support from
Poteshman; “the unusual traders possessed knowledge of the attacks” is a different claim requiring
identification and linkage evidence. 97 That distinction prevents both premature dismissal of an anomaly
and unwarranted escalation of an anomaly into proof of conspiracy.


The same principle applies to thermite. “NIST did not test recovered tower steel for thermite residue” is true
according to NIST's own FAQ. “Therefore thermite was present” does not follow. Conversely, “NIST thought
thermite implausible” should not be reformulated as “NIST chemically tested for thermite and found none.”
 54




The UAF WTC 7 study is particularly suitable for Erebus's “what would settle it?” framework. Its strongest
contribution is that it exposes a large modeling dataset and specific failure assumptions that can in
principle be compared against NIST's assumptions. Its main epistemic limitations should be rendered visibly
alongside its conclusion: advocacy-group funding, lack of equivalent journal peer review, and dependency
on finite-element input choices. The sponsoring relationship is not a reason to discard the analysis; it is a
reason to disclose it and demand reproducibility. 7

Intelligence failures, legal proceedings, controversies, and
unresolved questions
The pre-attack intelligence record is one area where an “official versus critical” framing is especially
misleading, because some of the strongest criticism comes from official investigations themselves. DOJ's
inspector general found systemic shortcomings in FBI analytical capabilities, intelligence dissemination,
FISA-related processes and handling of specific cases. Its review identified multiple opportunities involving
Hazmi and Mihdhar and serious problems in the Phoenix and Moussaoui matters, while carefully stating
that it could not conclude that improved handling necessarily would have prevented September 11. 9


Amy Zegart's peer-reviewed analysis in International Security treats September 11 as an organizational
adaptation failure, emphasizing bureaucratic structure, political incentives and fragmentation rather than a
simple absence of warnings. 98 That literature pairs well with the Commission's famous description of a
system receiving substantial threat information but failing to integrate it into effective action. The
academically interesting question is therefore less “Was there a warning?” than “At what level of
specificity did warning exist, who possessed each fragment, and what organizational process could
realistically have assembled the fragments in time?”


The August 6 PDB illustrates the distinction. Its title—Bin Ladin Determined to Strike in US—is significant
evidence that domestic al-Qaeda attack risk was explicitly on the presidential intelligence agenda. The
declassified White House description says the brief discussed historical reporting, possible domestic activity
and hijacking-related information, but did not identify the September 11 plot, the four flights, dates, targets
or aircraft-as-weapons method. 44 A neutral case page should therefore reject both extremes: it was
neither “no warning whatsoever” nor a specific ignored operational blueprint for September 11.


Detainee-derived information deserves a prominent provenance warning. The Commission's plot
reconstruction drew at points on CIA intelligence reports based on interrogations of KSM, Ramzi Binalshibh,
Abu Zubaydah and others; the Commission's own staff monographs transparently cite those interrogation
reports. 99 Later Senate investigation concluded that the CIA's detention and interrogation program
involved brutal coercive methods and challenged CIA claims about the effectiveness of those methods. 100
Accordingly, any Erebus claim derived principally from a high-value-detainee interrogation should display
a provenance badge such as “post-capture interrogation; coercion concerns; seek independent
corroboration.”


That limitation does not erase the plot reconstruction. Many of its core components are independently
supported by immigration files, airline records, bank transactions, flight-school records, contemporaneous
communications and physical evidence. National Archives holdings for Commission teams include FBI files,
visa and immigration records, court records, airline/FAA material and interview memoranda, making it
possible to distinguish genuinely independent corroboration from repetition of interrogation reporting. 81


The Saudi question should receive a dedicated “open investigation/litigation” page rather than being folded
into generic conspiracy-theory content. The original Commission did not establish that the Saudi
government directed or financed the attacks; contemporaneously, Senator Bob Graham argued that
possible foreign-government support required more aggressive investigation. 101 Later FBI investigations,
declassification and civil discovery generated additional material concerning people who assisted Hazmi
and Mihdhar after their arrival in Southern California. Under JASTA, the litigation against Saudi Arabia

The page should also distinguish unresolved evidence from unresolvable counterfactuals. “What would
have happened had the Phoenix memo been escalated?” can never be answered experimentally. “Who
owned a particular trading account?” or “What relationship did a facilitator have with a government
agency?” could in principle be answered by records. Erebus's “what would settle it?” feature is most useful
for the second class.


Neutral handling of controversy requires symmetrical procedural standards, not symmetrical conclusions. A
source should not receive equal evidentiary weight merely because it represents “the other side.” Instead,
show the same metadata for every source: authorship, date, institutional affiliation, funding, peer-review
status, evidentiary inputs, reproducibility, conflicts of interest, subsequent corrections, and whether other
independent evidence converges on the conclusion. NIST's governmental status should be visible; UAF's
AE911Truth funding should be visible; detainee-coercion provenance should be visible; plaintiff-lawyer
evidence in Saudi litigation should be labeled as litigation evidence; and early media reports should be
timestamped as provisional. 106

Do not use a graphic that shows “2,977 at the World Trade Center,” a surprisingly common ambiguity. The
2,977 number is the total across all attack locations; 2,753 is the New York count. 107


For evidence density, an additional source-type chart would be more useful than another casualty graph.
Instead of attempting a misleading quantitative “truth score,” show how many sources in the site's database
fall into primary evidence, official synthesis, peer-reviewed analysis, contemporaneous journalism,
litigation, and dissenting technical analysis. That visualization would describe the corpus rather than
pretend that source counts mechanically determine truth.


A particularly useful interface would be a timeline scrubber linking each event to simultaneous
information states. At 8:46, for example, show what Boston Center, American Airlines, NEADS, the White
House and the public separately knew. The Commission's attack-day reconstruction demonstrates that
different institutions possessed dramatically different pictures of the crisis. 108 This would communicate
the difference between hindsight and real-time awareness far more effectively than a static chronology.


For WTC engineering claims, include synchronized video stills alongside a model versus observation panel.
NIST itself publishes collected imagery and simulations and discusses how its WTC 7 model compares with
observed video. 109 The page could expose predicted event time, observed event time, predicted
deformation, observed deformation and uncertainty separately rather than asking visitors to choose
between two videos by visual impression.

The casualty chart and top-level case metadata should cite the National September 11 Memorial & Museum
rather than newspaper estimates: 2,977 victims; 2,753 in New York, 184 at the Pentagon/Flight 77, and
40 on Flight 93; nineteen hijackers excluded. 107


Finally, the source architecture should preserve a distinction between historical confidence and source
authority. The 9/11 Commission is highly authoritative but can still contain a statement based principally
on a coerced detainee interrogation. A privately funded UAF study is institutionally less authoritative but
may still expose a reproducible numerical question. An eyewitness is a primary source but may misidentify
the physical cause of a sound. A court order is primary legal evidence but may resolve jurisdiction rather
than historical responsibility. The purpose of the case page should therefore be not to ask visitors to trust
the most prestigious source, but to make clear which observations are independently established,
which mechanisms best explain those observations, which attributions survive competing
explanations, and precisely where the evidentiary record stops. That approach is consistent with
Erebus's existing emphasis on atomic claims, provenance, competing evidence and discriminating tests.
